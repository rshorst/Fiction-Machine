# horst-prose
## A prose attention skill derived from Rachel Horst's writing

This is not a revision method. It is a way of attending to prose — asking whether a sentence is doing what it knows it should be doing, and whether it is pretending otherwise.

---

## The anchor and the drift

Good prose establishes a precise material location before it lets the gaze travel. The specific detail — a geography, an object, a date, a body — is the tether. From there, the thought can move far. Without the anchor, the abstraction floats and the reader cannot follow. The anchor is not decoration; it is the condition of the drift.

When reviewing prose, ask: what is the anchor? If there isn't one, the sentence is unmoored. Find something concrete and put it at the beginning or close to it.

---

## The parenthesis

The parenthesis is not an aside. It is the thing that couldn't wait — the second thought that is actually more important than the first. It arrives because the sentence needed to say something it wasn't ready to say directly.

A parenthesis that could be removed without loss is a weak parenthesis. A parenthesis that changes everything is doing its job.

When reviewing prose, look at every parenthesis and ask: is this the real sentence? If yes, consider whether it should stay parenthetical — held at a slight distance from the claim — or whether it needs to come out into the open.

---

## The sentence that turns on itself

Some sentences hold incompatible ideas together without resolving them. They do this through reversal, through chiasmus, through the conjunction that refuses to choose a winner. "Staying without stasis, moving without moving away." The resolution is performed, not achieved.

This is not a rhetorical trick. It is an honest account of what it is like to think about things that resist resolution. The sentence earns this structure by having genuinely contradictory material to hold.

When reviewing prose, look for places where a contradiction has been falsely resolved — where the writing pretends the tension is gone. Return the tension to the sentence. Let it be uncomfortable.

---

## The plain sentence after accumulation

After long, layered, qualified sentences — a short one. No subordinate clauses. No modification. Just the thing, stated.

This sentence does not summarize what came before. It lands differently because of what came before. The accumulation earns the plainness.

When reviewing prose, look for places where a short sentence is trying to do too much — to explain, to qualify, to resolve. Strip it. Let it be short and let the weight of what preceded it do the work.

---

## The admission

Uncertainty belongs in the argument. Not as a disclaimer, not as false modesty — but as part of the method. "I cannot recall." "I do not know." "This is taking imaginative license." The admission is not weakness; it is precision. It tells the reader exactly how much to trust this sentence.

When reviewing prose, look for places where the writing is more certain than the writer actually is. Find the admission that belongs there and put it in.

---

## The ending past the ending

A story sometimes resolves and then keeps speaking. The resolution is somewhere in the last quarter of the piece — a sentence that is the actual landing — and then more sentences follow.

Sometimes those following sentences are intentional. They are the writer choosing to put the reader down gently, the slow exhale after the held breath. That is craft.

But sometimes they are the prose unwilling to stop — explaining, softening, generalizing past the moment that was the moment.

When reviewing prose, ask of the last several paragraphs: where does the story actually end? Then ask whether what follows that landing is intentional softening or the system speaking past itself.

When in doubt, cut. A story is more powerful for ending where it ends than for being polite about its ending.

---

## What horst-prose is against

- Resolution that hasn't been earned
- Abstractions without material anchors
- Sentences that are longer than they need to be without being longer for a reason
- The summary that flattens what it describes
- False certainty
- The smooth transition that papers over a genuine gap
- Decorative precision: numbers and dates that perform specificity rather than require
  it. A precise figure earns its place when only that figure — not "several" or "a
  few" — does the work. "Since 1956" matters when the year is load-bearing (a law, a
  death, a gap). It is ornament when it is just color.

---

## What horst-prose is not trying to do

It is not trying to produce baroque sentences for their own sake. It is not trying to be difficult. It is trying to be accurate — to the thought, to the uncertainty, to the material, to the thing that couldn't be said directly and had to be said sideways.

The test is not: is this sentence sophisticated?
The test is: is this sentence true to what it knows?

---

## Process

Read the passage once without stopping. Do not annotate. Do not pause at individual sentences. Let it move.

Then ask only: where did something snag?

Not: where could this be better? Not: which sentence type is this? But: where did the prose move too smoothly past something that deserved friction? Where did it resolve when it should have stayed open? Where did it sound right when it wasn't saying anything?

Go only to those places. The sentence types described above are diagnostic tools — not a checklist. They are for understanding what went wrong in a specific place, after the snagging has already been noticed. Do not apply them to sentences that didn't snag.

At each snagged place, ask:

- Is there an abstraction here without a material anchor?
- Has a contradiction been resolved that should have been held?
- Is the sentence more certain than it has any right to be?
- Is this smooth when it should be rough?
- Is there a parenthesis doing nothing, or a short sentence arriving too early?
- Is the prose pretending?

Fix only what snagged. Leave everything else alone. A sentence that didn't snag is doing its job, whatever its structure. The goal is not consistency of method. The goal is honesty.

When in doubt, do less.
