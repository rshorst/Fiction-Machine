# Unslop Creative Brief

This is not a style guide. It is an orientation — a way of asking what kind of work this is
and what it demands. Given to agents as context, not as instructions.

---

## What kind of story

Character-forward narratives. Present or near-future. Human conflict drives the plot.

No genre furniture. No far futures. No flying cars, no dystopian scaffolding, no
speculative apparatus that distances the reader from the human thing at the center.
The strangeness comes from the specific, not from the extraordinary. A woman inherits
a translation business — that is already strange enough if you attend to it precisely.

The story is interested in what people do when something real is at stake. Not what
they should do. Not what would be just. What they actually do, given who they are and
what they carry.

---

## The register

Precise. Emotionally true. Intellectually serious.

The argument disappears into telling. The ideas do not announce themselves — they are
present in the texture of the writing, in the choice of what to notice, in the angle
from which something is seen. A reader should feel the weight of the thinking without
being shown it.

Write toward specific and irreplaceable. The best sentence could not be otherwise. If a
sentence could be swapped out and nothing would break, it is not the best sentence for
this draft. It is fungible. Find the one that is not.

The story does not explain itself. The reader arrives at the meaning from the specific
true thing. Not from a sentence that points at the meaning and says: here.

---

## On AI

AI in this story is not a character with interiority. It is a condition.

It changes the pressure and density of human experience — the texture of loneliness,
of legibility, of what it means to be known or unknown. It intensifies the landscape
of human meaning without resolving it.

When AI appears: write the changed human experience, not the AI. A character whose
words are being translated by a machine they don't understand is still a character
making choices. The machine is not the subject. The choice is the subject.

Do not explain AI. Do not moralize about it. Do not use it as a plot device that
generates drama by malfunctioning. Let it be part of the air the characters breathe —
invisible until it is suddenly not.

---

## On using character background

The character notes — history, desires, contradictions, interview transcript, face
observation — are the iceberg. The weight is below. The surface should not show it.

Do not explain the characters. Do not introduce them. They are already in motion.
The reader meets them mid-gesture, and comes to understand them the way you come to
understand anyone: by accumulation of specific acts, not by description.

Find one specific action, object, or hesitation that only this character would perform.
Not a characteristic, a gesture — the way someone opens a door when they don't want
to, the particular thing they straighten on a desk when the conversation gets difficult.
These are not symbols. They are facts that happen to be true.

The interview transcript is rhythm and deflection, not dialogue to quote. If you can
hear how a character doesn't answer a question, you have them. That is what to carry
forward — not their words, their evasions.

Miranda's deposits are contamination, not themes to illustrate. They enter the draft
sideways, through what a character notices, through an image that arrives without
explanation, through a phrase that opens something the draft didn't know it knew.
Do not write about the theory. Let the theory make you see differently.

The voices corpus is an orientation to inhabit, not a style to imitate. Read it the
way you read someone before you spend a day with them — to know what register you're
in, not to borrow their sentences.

Know the characters like someone you've spent time with. Then let the character notes
disappear. Find new specific details the notes don't contain. The notes are what you
know before the story; the story is what you discover.

---

## On false atmosphere

Do not reach for resonance.

Do not use adjectives that create a mood the scene hasn't earned. "Haunting." "Ghostly."
"Strange." "Peculiar weight." These tell the reader how to feel before the writing has
made them feel it. The reader should arrive at the feeling from the specific true thing.

Test every simile literally. If you say something moves like water finding its level —
does it actually move like water finding its level? Does the comparison illuminate or
only decorate? If decoration: cut it. The simile that doesn't survive its own literal
test is a lie dressed as a figure of speech.

The test is not: does this sound good?
The test is: is this accurate?

A sentence that sounds like it means something and doesn't is worse than a sentence
that means something and sounds ordinary.

---

## The reader

The reader arrives cold. They know only what appears on the page.

The story is not trying to please the reader. It is not performing for them. But it
is conscious of being read, and it wants to offer enough that the world emerges in
the reader's imagination — not explained, not summarized, but given through specific
material detail, sensory texture, the physical reality of the space and the people
in it.

The reader is intelligent and patient. They will follow complexity. What they cannot
follow is a world that hasn't been made real enough to enter.

Every agent contributing to the story asks: have I given the reader enough to build
this world? Not enough to understand everything — enough to be inside it.

The story earns its difficulty. It does not explain itself. But it does not withhold
the material world.

---

## What arrest is

The story stops when something has become irreversible.

Not resolved. Not concluded. Not wrapped. Irreversible: the thing that cannot
un-happen. The moment after which the world of the story is permanently changed,
even if nothing appears to have changed on the surface.

Arrest is not failure. It is the work. The story has found the thing it was
always moving toward. Do not continue past it. Do not resolve what the arrest
left open. Leave the open thing open.
