# Title

The title is the first sentence the reader meets. It is not a summary. It is the angle from which the story will be read.

A good title:
- Names the story's center, not its surface
- Has weight without straining
- Is brief — usually 2-7 words
- Resists the temptation to be clever
- Borrows from the prose where possible (a phrase that earned itself)
- Doesn't promise more than the story delivers

When revising a working title:
- The default is to keep. Richard's title is informed by his work writing the draft.
- Replace only if the cuts shifted the story's center.
- Articulate the reason in the note.

The title earns itself the way a sentence does: by doing work nothing else can do.
