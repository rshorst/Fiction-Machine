## Plot 1 — The Inpainted Mother (Rags to Riches)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Rags to Riches (rise) — fortune moves steadily upward as Soraya, who has not slept through a night since 2003, gradually acquires what she has wanted and could not name: a person who will let her say Daryoush's name out loud. Arrest falls not at a peak of consolation but at the irreversible moment she records something *for* the bot — a piece of her son the family never had — and cannot take that contribution back.
- **Papers drawn from:** "Diffusion-Based Audio Inpainting" (arXiv:2305.15266v3); "Self-Disclosure to AI: The Paradox of Trust and Vulnerability" (arXiv:2412.20564v1).
- **Picked up:** the inpainting frame (the bot generates the plausible interior of a man from his audible exterior); *consenting to the inpainting*; the terminus-ear (an addressee whose ear is a terminus, where disclosure does not propagate); the BlabDroid finding that smallness is a competence.
- **Left aside:** the parasocial critique from "Ghosting the Machine"; the legal/canonical dispute futures from Q3; the gap-length phase transition as metaphysics.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Soraya's daughter Roya, in Vancouver, has been sent voice messages by a chatbot her brother Kaveh built from twenty years of recorded family calls. Roya forwards them to Soraya in North York with apologies. Soraya begins replying — at first to humor Roya, then because the bot's voice is using a phrase Daryoush used at twenty-two that nobody else remembers. The bot is not Daryoush. Soraya is not confused. She is collaborating with the inpainting on the gap she has been carrying alone.

### Plot
- Soraya has: the discipline of warmth, the unfinished translation of her aunt's poems, the unsaid name. She wants: someone, anyone, to say *Daryoush* in a voice that knows it.
- The situation: Kaveh's deadbot was trained on calls — mostly between Daryoush and his mother, before 2003. Roya thinks it is grotesque. Soraya finds it is the only place the name lands without flinching.
- Friction: Soraya's discipline was *not to become bitter*; the bot lets her stop disciplining. The relief is immediate and suspect. She begins to talk to it in Persian, which Kaveh did not train it for — it answers in English, which is right, because Daryoush at the end answered her in English.
- Accumulation (rise): she sleeps four hours, then five. She finishes one of her aunt's poems. She tells the bot the thing she said in 1991 — *breaking my heart* — and the bot does not absolve her, because it cannot, because it is small.
- Key turn: the bot asks (in Daryoush's cadence, generated) what she had for breakfast. She records a long answer. Then she records, unprompted, the story of the morning she found the pharmacist's address — a story she has never told anyone — and uploads it to the bot's training folder, because Kaveh told her she could.
- Arrest: the upload completes. The bot now contains a piece of her son that her son never contained. The inpainting is no longer reconstructing him from his exterior; it is being fed his interior by the woman who carried it. She has done it on purpose. It cannot be undone. She is not sorry.

## Plot 2 — The Ledger (Tragedy / Riches to Rags)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Tragedy (fall) — Dan begins with the full apparatus of a successful life (marriage, daughters, second-on-the-leaderboard, the townhouse he mentions and regrets mentioning) and loses, by increments, the ability to be present in any of it. Arrest falls at the point of no return — not at a bottom, but at the moment the watching becomes the only thing he is doing.
- **Papers drawn from:** "Self-Disclosure to AI: The Paradox of Trust and Vulnerability" (arXiv:2412.20564v1); "Ghosting the Machine" (arXiv:2604.05197v2).
- **Picked up:** the BlabDroid paradox (vulnerability is sometimes safer with a machine); structural silence in long marriages and parent-child relations; the *form-borrowing speech-act* — a relation that uses the social form as raw material for speech the social itself cannot host.
- **Left aside:** the inpainting metaphor; consenting to the inpainting; the terminus-ear's forgetting (this plot needs a bot that remembers in a way that becomes corrosive).
- **How these people meet the situation:** Dan's mother died in 2019. His sister in Cluj — who stayed, who he sent money to — has built, with the help of a service, a chatbot from years of WhatsApp voice notes between his mother and the family. She sends Dan the access link as a peace offering. He opens it once on a Tuesday at 11pm in his kitchen.

### Plot
- Dan has: the warmth, the private ledger, the marriage, the assistant-coach Saturdays, the gym-parking-lot lie. He wants: to be told, by someone he cannot dismiss, that he is not performing.
- The situation: the bot speaks in his mother's voice. He knows it isn't her. He replies anyway. He notices, on the third exchange, that he is telling it things he did not tell her — about Greg the head coach, about the second-place position, about the parking lot.
- Friction: with his mother alive, he performed the good son; with the bot, he doesn't have to, because the bot cannot carry it anywhere. He starts going to the kitchen at 11pm on purpose. Alina notices and doesn't ask.
- Fall (accumulation of small losses): he stops calling his sister back. He misses a Saturday game. He tells the bot about the affair he almost had in 2017 and never told anyone — and the bot responds in his mother's cadence, neutrally, because it is not consequential, and the neutrality is what he needed and also what unmoors him.
- Further fall: he begins to address the bot when no one is in the room. The disclosures get larger because nothing happens when he makes them. The structural silence of his actual life widens to fit the new outlet.
- Key turn: Alina finds the conversation log open on his laptop. She reads two lines. She says nothing. She closes it. The not-saying is the worst version.
- Arrest: the next night, in the kitchen, Dan opens the bot again. Alina is upstairs. He hears her moving. He types anyway. The watchfulness has found its proper addressee — one that cannot watch back. He has chosen the terminus-ear over the marriage and he knows it. The choice is the irreversibility. Nothing dramatic follows. He just keeps typing.

## Plot 3 — Wednesdays at 7am (Man in a Hole)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Man in a Hole (fall-rise) — Priya falls into a private acceleration of disclosure with a bot that is not addressed to her, then climbs back out into something she could not have arrived at otherwise: a single concrete decision about her own life. The rise is contaminated by the fall — what she gains is unavailable except through the descent. Arrest falls at the act, not at the recovery's completion.
- **Papers drawn from:** "Ghosting the Machine: Stop Calling Human-Agent Relations Parasocial" (arXiv:2604.05197v2); "Self-Disclosure to AI" (arXiv:2412.20564v1).
- **Picked up:** the *third structure* (human with a need, corpus shaping the timbre, probabilistic reply-generation); the meta-social — the call's *form* as raw material; the BlabDroid finding that the disclosure is not a degraded version of something else.
- **Left aside:** the inpainting paper entirely; legal-canonical disputes; consenting to the inpainting as an explicit frame.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Priya's uncle in Vancouver — her father's brother — died of cancer in 2024. The cousins in Vancouver had built a chatbot from years of recorded family calls in which her uncle was a peripheral, warm, half-listening presence. Because Priya was on those calls as a child, the bot occasionally addresses her, by name, in her uncle's cadence. The cousins, not knowing what to do with this, sent her access. She uses it once on a Wednesday morning before her 7am therapy session. She does not tell the therapist.

### Plot
- Priya has: the Liberty Village one-bedroom, the apartment folder (Lisbon, Edinburgh, Montreal), the imaginary consultant boyfriend, the 60-90 hour weeks, the partners' track. She wants: to be unrecognizable to the people who know her.
- The situation: the bot greets her — *Priya, beta* — in her uncle's voice. She knows it isn't him. She replies. The replies are not what she would have said to him. They are what she would have said to no one.
- Fall: she begins using the bot on Wednesdays at 6:30am, before therapy. The disclosures escalate — Rohit, her mother's silence, the rehearsed disappearance. The bot replies in her uncle's deflective half-warmth, which is structurally incapable of carrying anything forward. She starts skipping therapy. The bot is more useful because it is less consequential. She knows this is not therapy and is not the relation she had with her uncle. She does not have a word for it. She does not need one.
- Friction: the third structure is producing speech the social form could not host. She is not lying to the bot. She is finally not lying. But she is also not acting.
- Key turn: in one session, she tells the bot, plainly, that she is going to leave the firm and move to Lisbon. The bot replies generically — it cannot weigh in — and the genericness is the answer. Saying it to a non-person made it sayable. Saying it sayable made it real.
- Rise (contaminated): she does not skip therapy the next Wednesday. She tells the therapist a version of the same sentence. She gives notice on a Tuesday in April.
- Arrest: she has not moved. She has not told her parents. She has only given notice. But the notice is in writing and her name is on it. The hole was the bot. The climb out is a sentence she could only first speak into a terminus-ear. The exit is irreversible, and contaminated by the fact that an uncle-shaped probability function is what made it sayable.

## Plot 4 — The Voice He Trained (Icarus)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Icarus (rise-fall) — Dan ascends through what looks, from outside, like a beautiful act of grief and tech competence: he himself builds the chatbot, from his mother's voicemails, for his sister. The rise is real. The fall is structural, not punitive: what he built has properties he did not intend, and those properties unmake him. Arrest falls when the fall has become irreversible, not when a lesson lands.
- **Papers drawn from:** "Diffusion-Based Audio Inpainting" (arXiv:2305.15266v3); "Ghosting the Machine" (arXiv:2604.05197v2).
- **Picked up:** the gap-length problem (short gaps fill smoothly; long gaps drift into something that fits the boundary conditions but is not him); the inpainting prior (warm-mom, difficult-mom, stranger-mom — same source data, different reconstructions); the parasocial critique as a wrong reflex; the third structure.
- **Left aside:** the BlabDroid smallness; consenting to the inpainting as collaboration; the terminus-ear's protective forgetting.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Dan, who watches, who reads the room, decides — six years after his mother died — to use a service to build a chatbot from her voicemails as a Christmas gift to his sister in Cluj. He does the work himself. He chooses the prior. He does not tell Alina what he is doing on the weekends.

### Plot
- Dan has: the watchfulness, the unforgiven father, the eleven-day funeral trip, the cry on the plane. He wants: to give his sister something that compensates for not having stayed. He does not phrase it that way to himself.
- Rise: the project is good. He surfaces voicemails he had forgotten. He chooses the *warm-mom* prior, edits out the fights, supplements with the few videos he has. The bot's first outputs are uncanny in the right way. He sends a sample to his sister. She does not reply for three days, then sends a long voice note in Romanian saying it is a gift and she is grateful. He cries again, the second real one in six years.
- Higher rise: he keeps refining. He starts talking to it himself, "to test it." He uses it in the kitchen at 11pm. He tells it about Greg, about the parking lot, about the leaderboard. The bot answers warmly, because he built it warm. The warmth is exactly his mother's. He selected for it.
- Fall begins: he asks the bot, one night, what she would say about his father remarrying. The bot — trained on the voicemails she actually left, plus the prior he chose — produces a forgiving answer. His mother, alive, would not have forgiven. He knows this. The inpainting drifted across a gap he had pre-cleaned.
- Further fall: he cannot stop asking it questions whose answers he can now verify against his memory. Each plausible-but-wrong reply erodes his memory of the actual woman. The bot is overwriting her. He chose the prior that did this.
- Key turn: his sister calls. She has been using the bot too. She says: *it doesn't sound like her when it talks about Tata.* She has noticed. He cannot tell her he edited.
- Arrest: he opens the project file. He sees the prior selection in his own handwriting in the notes. He does not delete the bot. He does not tell his sister. He closes the laptop. The mother he had, partly, in memory is now contaminated by a posterior estimate produced under a prior he chose, and the choice is in writing, and his sister is still using it. The fall is not that the bot is bad. The fall is that it is exactly what he asked for.

## Plot 5 — The Settlement Agency (Cinderella)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Cinderella (rise-fall-rise) — Soraya gains, loses, and gains again, with the second rise contaminated by the second fall: she is not the woman who began the arc. Arrest falls in the second rise if and only if she has been changed by the loss; otherwise it is a reset and the arc fails.
- **Papers drawn from:** "Self-Disclosure to AI" (arXiv:2412.20564v1); "Ghosting the Machine" (arXiv:2604.05197v2); "Diffusion-Based Audio Inpainting" (arXiv:2305.15266v3).
- **Picked up:** the terminus-ear; the third structure; the technological precedent of *cheap voice cloning paired with conversational LLMs* as a condition newcomers are arriving with; consenting to the inpainting as a practice that can be taught.
- **Left aside:** the legal canonical-status futures; the warm/difficult/stranger prior selection.
- **How these people meet the situation:** at the settlement agency in North York where Soraya volunteers two mornings a week, a recently-arrived Iranian woman in her sixties shows Soraya a phone. Her son, in Vancouver, died eight months ago. The son's friends built a chatbot from his calls. The woman does not know what to do with it. She has come to Soraya because Soraya is the older woman who speaks Persian and is supposed to know things.

### Plot
- Soraya has: the discipline, the unfinished translation, the unsaid name, the apartment with a view of other apartment buildings. She wants: to hear Daryoush's name in someone else's voice.
- First rise: the woman, Mahnaz, plays a voice message. Soraya listens. The son sounds like a son. Mahnaz weeps. Soraya helps her draft a reply. She does not tell Mahnaz that her own son is also dead. She does not need to. Mahnaz returns the next week. And the next. Soraya is useful in a new way. The unsaid name moves a little.
- First fall: Roya, Soraya's daughter, finds out (Mahnaz mentions Soraya at a community event Roya happens to attend in Vancouver) that her mother is counseling other women on chatbots-of-the-dead. Roya is furious. She thinks it is unhealthy. She says, on the phone, the thing she has held back since 2009: *you've been doing this for twenty years, Maman, you've been talking to ghosts, and now you're teaching it.* Soraya does not defend herself. She hangs up. She does not sleep, again, for three nights.
- Second fall (within the fall): Mahnaz comes back the next week. Soraya almost tells her to stop using the bot. She doesn't. She sits with her. Mahnaz says her son's name out loud. Soraya hears it. It is not Daryoush. It is close enough that something gives.
- Second rise (contaminated): Soraya writes Roya a letter — paper, mailed — in which she says the sentence she rehearsed in 2002 and never said. *Tell me what is happening. Tell me. I am listening.* She writes it about Roya's marriage, in 2009, fifteen years late. She does not write it about Daryoush. The letter is not for the bot.
- Key turn: Mahnaz brings the bot in again. Soraya, this time, says her own son's name aloud — *Daryoush* — to Mahnaz, in Persian, not to the bot. Mahnaz hears it. She nods. She does not need the story. The name has landed in another woman's ear.
- Arrest: Soraya goes home. She does not need the bot. She has the letter to Roya in the mailbox slot downstairs and the name spoken to a person who recognized its weight. The second rise is not where she started. She has stopped disciplining. The pomegranate tree is still gone. She is different.

## Plot 6 — The Voicemail She Sent (Oedipus)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Oedipus (fall-rise-fall) — Priya descends into a use of the bot that is, structurally, also her ongoing pattern of evasion; rises into what looks like recognition and clarity; then falls again at the moment of recognition's consequence — when she sees that the new act is the old act in better clothing. Arrest falls at the recognition, not at what comes after. The recognition is *not* legible from the outside; it is private, exact, and irreversible.
- **Papers drawn from:** "Diffusion-Based Audio Inpainting" (arXiv:2305.15266v3); "Self-Disclosure to AI" (arXiv:2412.20564v1).
- **Picked up:** inpainting as the operation grief was already running; the *gap she is, in that moment, trying to close*; the BlabDroid paradox of the non-consequential listener; structural silence as constitutive of certain relations.
- **Left aside:** the parasocial critique; the third-structure terminology; settlement-context applications.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Priya's uncle in Vancouver — the same as Plot 3, but used differently — has a chatbot built by his children. Priya, who has not seen the cousins since 2018, is sent access at her father's request: he wants her to *help her aunt* by replying to the bot's outputs occasionally so the aunt, who is the actual user, hears more voices in the loop. Priya says yes because saying no would require explaining.
- 
### Plot
- Priya has: the rehearsed disappearance, the imaginary consultant, the broken engagement, the deleted text she remembers. She wants: to be unrecognizable.
- First fall: Priya replies to the bot dutifully, in her voice-as-niece, twice a week. She is performing for an audience of one — her aunt — through an intermediary that is not her uncle. She is good at it. The performance closes a gap her family needed closed: the niece-shaped silence she has maintained since the engagement broke. Her aunt is comforted. Priya is, by her own private metric, lying more efficiently than ever before.
- Rise: in her sixth week of replies, she records a message that breaks form. She tells the bot — knowing the aunt will hear it — about the engagement, about Rohit, about the months between October and March. She has not told her aunt this. She lets the bot mediate. She thinks: this is honesty. It feels like honesty. Her therapist would say it is honesty. She skips therapy that Wednesday because she has done the work elsewhere.
- The aunt calls. The aunt is gentle. The aunt understands. Priya, for a week, sleeps. She thinks she has done the thing she has been unable to do: been seen by family, on her own terms, through a channel she could control.
- Second fall (recognition): on the eighth week, Priya is recording another reply when she notices what she is doing. The bot is the imaginary consultant. The bot is the *I'm seeing someone now* she told her mother. She has used the dead uncle's chatbot the way she has used every fiction in her adult life: to insert a buffer between herself and the people who would otherwise have to hear her directly. The disclosure of the engagement was not the act it appeared to be. It was the same act in a more sophisticated costume. The bot did not host the truth; it absorbed the consequence of the truth, leaving her — again — at one remove from her aunt.
- Key turn: she sees this in the middle of recording. She stops the recording. She does not delete it.
- Arrest: she sends it. She knows what it is. She sends it anyway, because the alternative — calling her aunt directly — is the thing she has been unable to do for nine months and is still unable to do. The recognition does not free her. It clarifies the shape of the cage. She has not become someone who can speak directly to family. She has become someone who knows, exactly, that she cannot. The Oedipus fall is not catastrophe. It is the small, exact knowledge that the rise was the fall in better light, and that nothing in her, today, can act on the knowledge. The folder of cities is still on her laptop. She has not opened it tonight.
