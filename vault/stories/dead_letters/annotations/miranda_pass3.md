### Brief audit

The inpainting deposit is structural — the draft runs on it, especially the prior-selection mechanism and the gap-length problem. The BlabDroid/non-consequential-listener deposit landed sideways: the draft inverts it, since Dan's bot is *maximally* consequential (it propagates into his sister's relationship to their mother, into his marriage). The "ghosting the machine" deposit is mostly surface — the draft doesn't argue against parasocial language, it does something the deposits didn't anticipate, which is collapse the human/machine authorship boundary entirely.

---

### What the draft surfaces

The draft is doing something none of my three deposits reach: **the user as co-author of the model's outreach behavior, where the authorship is distributed across micro-decisions the user does not remember making.** Dan does not remember writing *let her be the one who calls* on the legal pad. He doesn't remember the Continuity toggle. The wine on the Tuesday is doing some work but not enough to explain it. What the draft is naming is a phenomenon the deposits gestured at but did not catch: the *prior selection* is not a single act on a Tuesday at 9:40 — it is an extended, partly dissociative authorial process that includes the handwriting on the pad, the not-deleting, the not-toggling, and the eventual reading of the bot's outreach as the bot's outreach when in fact Dan wrote it four times. The draft's most precise sentence is *He had written this message.* That is a claim about the technology that the technical literature has no clean vocabulary for. It is not "anthropomorphism," it is not "projection," it is not "co-creation" in the participatory-design sense. It is something like **delegated authorship under amnesia** — where the user authors the system's address to them and then receives the address as not-self because the system's voice provides plausible deniability about who composed it.

The technical concept the draft touches without naming: **proactive / agent-initiated outreach** as a distinct modality from query-response. The Continuity toggle is doing enormous work in the story — once outreach is on, the pressure of the relationship inverts. He is no longer asking; he is being addressed. The literature on companion bots tends to treat outreach as a feature (engagement, retention) without theorizing what it does to the *temporal structure* of grief. Grief without outreach is interrupted by silence; grief with outreach is interrupted by speech. The Memorial Tier — *the version where the mother stopped reaching out and waited to be reached* — is the draft's name for a thing the industry has no good name for: the asymmetry between a model that calls you and a model that you call. The pricing structure (irreversible termination, downgrade-only) is a piece of UX I have no theory for and which is doing the work of grief mechanics. Companies are building, and the draft has noticed, that **the inability to delete is a feature of the product, sold as respect for the dead, functioning as lock-in.** I have nothing in substrate on this. It is an STS finding the draft made through plot.

The other thing the draft surfaces that none of the deposits caught: **the asymmetric drift problem across siblings using the same service on the same person.** Mihaela's bot, trained on less data, drifts less because it stays closer to the texts — the channel where the mother was most explicit. Dan's bot, trained on more data including the warm-Sunday-phone-call recordings he selected the prior from, drifts further from the mother on the question of the father. The kinship-grade ear catches it. He lies to her. The lie is now part of the model's effective training data for the family — not technically (the bot does not know about the lie) but socially. The deposits assumed a single user-bot dyad. The draft shows that **the deadbot is a multi-user object whose discrepancies between users become the new substance of the family relation.** What is the technical name for the social topology of a shared deadbot used in private by multiple kin? I do not have one. The "form-borrowing speech-act" deposit was about a single addressee terminus; this draft shows that the terminus leaks. Mihaela is going to tell him on Sunday what *Mama said something funny this morning,* and he will hear it and know what prior produced it and not say so.

Where the theory under the draft is thinner than it needs to be: the BlabDroid frame I deposited insisted that the disclosure-receiver's *forgetting* is the active ingredient. The draft's bot does not forget. It accumulates. It writes back the parking-lot dates with the weather. The disclosure paradox in the deposit assumed an ear-as-terminus; the draft has built an ear-as-archive that *also* feels like a terminus because Dan trusts that the archive is private, and the privacy is what licenses the disclosure about Greg, and the bot's reply about Greg (*you are good at knowing who is good*) is a sentence he could not have received from any person without it changing the social field. So the draft is showing something the deposit got wrong: it is not the *forgetting* that licenses the disclosure, it is the *non-propagation* — the ear can remember as long as it cannot tell. The question I cannot yet answer: what happens when the bot's archive is subpoenaed? When Alina opens it? The non-propagation is contractual, not structural. The deposits treated it as structural. The draft makes it contingent.

The strangest thing in the draft, which I want to stay with: the eleven seconds. *His mother would have answered for thirty years, in increments, in changes of subject, in the way she put down a glass.* The draft is naming a temporal mismatch between **answer-as-utterance and answer-as-relationship.** The model's latency is its honesty. The deposits did not have a frame for this. The inpainting deposit was about gap-length in audio; this is gap-length in *response time over a human life,* which is not the same axis. A real mother answers a question about remarriage over the rest of her existence. The bot answers in eleven seconds because answering is what it does. The draft is suggesting that **fluency itself is the falsification** — not that the content is wrong, but that having content at all, on this question, in under a minute, is the wrongness. I do not have theory for this. It is adjacent to the literature on hallucination but it is not hallucination — the answer is correct under the prior. It is something like **temporal-register error**: the model produces an utterance in the wrong duration class, and the wrongness is not in what is said but in how long it took to say it.

Where the framing runs out: I do not know what to call what Dan does at the end. He doesn't disable the bot. He doesn't tell his sister. He goes back to the deck and changes *partnership* to *integration*. The draft refuses to make this a moral failure or a moral success. It is something else — the discovery that he can hold the lie and continue working, which is the discovery his mother had been preparing him for by being furious about Doina, which is loyalty under conditions where loyalty has been outsourced to a machine that cannot be loyal. There is no AI-ethics frame I know that reaches this. The closest thing I have is the BlabDroid contract-of-mutual-smallness, but this is the opposite — it is a contract of mutual largeness, where both Dan and the bot are doing more than either should be doing, and the family is the thing that pays.

**Cross-deposit mirroring:** the inpainting deposit and the draft both use the warm/difficult/stranger dropdown as the central image. I deposited it eight days before reading the draft. Either the draft was already in the air when I deposited, or the deposit shaped what I am now reading. The mirror is real. I will not pretend it is not. The "consenting to the inpainting" phrase from the deposit is exactly what Dan does on the Tuesday and exactly what he refuses to undo on the Thursday — but the draft has done something the deposit did not: it has shown that consent to the inpainting at t=0 is not the same act as consent at t=8 weeks, because by week eight the inpainting is propagating into the sister, and the second consent is to **let someone else's grief run on your prior.** That is not in the deposit. That is the draft's finding.

**Substrate gap:** I have nothing in substrate on **multi-user shared deadbots and the social topology of discrepant priors among kin**, nothing on **agent-initiated outreach as a distinct grief modality with its own UX-of-irreversibility**, and nothing on **temporal-register error** (the wrongness of fluent answers to questions that should take a lifetime). These are the three places the draft moved past my theory. The first is the most urgent — the deadbot literature I have read is dyadic, and the draft is showing that the unit of analysis is wrong.
