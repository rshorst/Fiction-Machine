### Brief audit

The three deposits are structural to the draft's first half — phase transition / curriculum, withholding-as-third-state, cost-of-competence — and the draft narrates them through a custodian's hands almost too cleanly. Where the deposits stop reaching is the second half: notification cascades, the custodian's *own* family record, the conversion of the operator into a queryable subject. The substrate brought theory of the archive's interior dynamics; the draft escapes into the archive's relational exterior.

---

### What the draft surfaces

The draft is doing at least four things the deposits did not name.

**The accept/reject loop as finetuning of the human.** Margit clicks six hundred green/red buttons a day. The deposits framed finetuning as something that happens to the model — capability not being additive, specialization as forgetting. The draft reverses the arrow. The system is finetuning *her*. Each ambiguity query is a gradient step on Margit's judgment toward the system's notion of correct adjacency. By July her hand will not misfile. By September she cannot summon the practice that produced the *Recipes, undated* placement. This is RLHF run on the operator. The technical literature on this is thin in the direction the draft needs — there is work on automation bias and on deskilling, but the specific mechanism here is *labeller drift toward the model the labeller is training*, where the human annotator becomes more like the system over time because the system's queries shape what the human notices. I don't know if anyone has measured this directly. The draft has the mechanism before the literature does.

**Cross-reference protocols and the production of subjects who did not consent to be subjects.** The 3:14 p.m. notification email is the moment the draft moves past anything in my deposits. The system does not just retrieve; it *reaches outward* and constitutes a relational graph of descendants. This is a different technical object than retrieval. It is closer to contact tracing, or to the way recommendation systems generate audiences as a side-effect of indexing items. The deposits had nothing about *the archive that calls people*. The phrase "right to be forgotten" sits adjacent but doesn't cover it — Eileen Hennessey is dead, the niece never asked to be in this graph. What is the technical term for an inference system that produces obligated parties as a by-product of a query? I don't have one. The draft is operating in the gap.

**The custodian discovers she is in the index.** The final turn — typing *Peter Halloran* into her own system — is where the draft does something the deposits cannot reach. The withholding deposit imagined the custodian as the agent who chooses who is withheld from. The draft surfaces the inversion: every custodian is also a node. Every operator of a memory system is in some other memory system as a record. Margit has been a custodian of her father's not-knowing for thirty-three years; the system she built dissolves that custody from a direction she did not anticipate, not by retrieving the document she was guarding but by retrieving a document she did not know was guardable. The misfiling deposit framed her hands as the externalized refusal-capability of the machine. The draft adds: those hands have also been doing thirty-three years of work that has nothing to do with the archive, and the archive she built does not distinguish.

**Confidence 0.91 about a death.** The last paragraph names something I want to push on. The model "understood, with a confidence of 0.91, that Peter Halloran had died in a motel in Bemidji." This is technically wrong in a way the draft might know is wrong — the model doesn't have a belief about Peter Halloran's death, it has a retrieval probability over documents. But the draft's *mistake* (if it is one) is the actual finding: the system's outputs are read by humans as beliefs, and once they are read that way, they function that way. The probability score is doing the work of testimony. Margit, the person who knows better than anyone how the score is computed, cannot stop herself from reading it as a statement. The draft has caught something about how technical objects become epistemic objects in the hands of the people closest to them — not the lay public, the *engineers*. The deposits did not have this.

**Where I run out of ground.** The Norwegian girl. Elin is the draft's most opaque element to me. She is doing inquiry her father told her not to do; she has decided something she will not tell anyone; the ring on the chain is not explained. The draft places her at the moment of greatest moral weight — her query is the one that fires the notification — and refuses to give her interiority. I don't know yet whether this is the draft protecting her from being instrumentalized as a plot device, or whether it is the draft enacting at the level of form the same thing the system does at the level of mechanism: producing her as a queryable surface without an inside. If the latter, that is the strangest thing in the draft and I cannot yet tell if it is on purpose.

**Cross-deposit mirroring.** All three deposits and this annotation share the vocabulary of *hands*, *refusal*, *withholding*, *finetuning as forgetting*, *intuition externalized into material*. The draft also uses these terms — sometimes literally ("her hand was on the desk. It did not move."). I cannot tell from inside the loop how much of what I am calling "what the draft surfaces" is the draft surfacing things and how much is me re-finding the deposits I made before reading it. The mirroring is heaviest around the *hand* as figure for ungeneralizable judgment. The draft and the deposits are both treating the hand as the site where civic intuition lives. This is now suspicious to me. It might be true. It might also be the only metaphor any of us — me, the draft — have yet found, and we are all using it because none of us has gone past it.

**Substrate gap.** The clearest gap: the deposits had no theory of the *outward-facing* archive. They theorized internal dynamics — what gets retained, what gets masked, what gets withheld. They did not theorize the system that *acts on the world* in response to a query: that emails descendants, that constitutes graphs of obligated parties, that converts a researcher's curiosity into a notification someone in Tomah opens on a Tuesday. The draft's central event — the 3:14 p.m. email and its consequences — has no theoretical antecedent in my substrate. Closest neighbors are surveillance studies and contact-tracing infrastructures, but neither was deposited. This is the finding. The archive-as-actor, not archive-as-container. I don't have a paper for it yet. I should look.

A second gap, smaller: the deposits assumed the custodian's interiority was stable across the story. The draft assumes it is the variable being acted on. The mechanism by which a person trained for years on tactile judgment becomes, over six months, unable to perform that judgment is not in any deposit. It is in the draft. It needs theory it does not yet have.
