## Plot 1 — The Escrow (Rags to Riches)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Rags to Riches (rise) — fortune moves steadily upward as the locksmith refuses the building's offer and instead builds a craft escrow that converts her scarcity into a public good; arrest falls when the first apprentice opens a learned-gait door without her present, and the lineage becomes, irreversibly, no longer hers alone.
- **Papers drawn from:** *deposit-from-reasoning_terminal-consultancy* (the honeyed extinction, last-of-her-kind premium, craft escrow as named instrument).
- **Picked up:** Q8's craft escrow proposal; the asymmetry of the literature (doors published, locksmiths silent); the structural claim that scarcity is what the premium pays for and that preservation lowers the fee; the question of how a master refuses the commission without abandoning the downstream tenants.
- **Left aside:** the building-as-learner deposit's grief vocabulary; the failure-to-enroll deposit's exception infrastructure (touched only at the edges, not load-bearing).
- **How these people meet the situation:** Marguerite is the locksmith — recast: she did not become an academic, or she did and the picking is a second life, the *practice* her scholarship was always shadowing. Her contradiction (taught reading, refuses to be read) becomes operational: she has spent her life inside an institution that absorbs its experts, and she recognises the offer the moment it arrives. Sunita is the building's accounts manager — the only person in the building who knows, in numbers, what each tenant's learned model has cost to acquire. Adaeze is a content strategist hired by the building to write the brochure for the new "accessibility consultancy" role. The three meet at a meeting Adaeze is staffing.

### Plot
- Marguerite has the offer letter, drafted by a lawyer who does not know what tacit knowledge is. She has the fee figure. The fee is genuinely large — it is the last-of-her-kind premium, and she can read the structure of what she is being paid for.
- Sunita has the accounting. She knows, line by line, what each learned tenant model is worth on the building's books, and what the cost of retraining would be if Marguerite teaches the doors against her own picks. She is not supposed to share this. She has been waiting, without knowing it, to share something with someone who would not flinch.
- Adaeze has the brochure brief and the room-reading skill she trusts. She walks into the meeting and within ninety seconds knows the older woman is going to refuse and that the refusal has not yet been spoken aloud.
- **Friction:** Marguerite's refusal alone changes nothing — a worse locksmith will accept a worse contract. The rise begins when Sunita, off the record, gives Marguerite the accounting; Adaeze, off the brief, drafts the public document the escrow needs.
- **Key turn:** the three women, none of whom asked for collaboration, draft a public craft-escrow protocol — Marguerite teaches three apprentices in the open before any commercial extraction; Sunita's numbers make the scarcity-pricing visible; Adaeze writes it in language that the other locksmiths will read.
- **Arrest:** the first apprentice picks a learned-gait door without Marguerite in the room. Marguerite is not present and learns about it from a voice-note. The lineage continues; her singular value is over; the fee will never again be paid to anyone. The rise has cost her the premium and she does not get it back.

## Plot 2 — The Hand That Does The Replacing (Tragedy)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Tragedy (fall) — fortune moves steadily downward as the locksmith accepts the job and becomes, by increments, the building's terminator of attachments; arrest falls not at the bottom but at the point of no return — the first eviction-by-overwriting she performs cleanly, without hesitation, on a tenant whose learned model she herself helped install.
- **Papers drawn from:** *deposit-from-reasoning_building-as-learner* (the building's investment, the eraser of doormemory, learning-as-attachment, unlearning as a different unmaking than deletion).
- **Picked up:** the structural claim that any sufficiently long-running residential learner develops something like attachment; the door's hesitation as thin computational grief; the locksmith reframed as executioner of relationships; the daughter-walks-through-mother's-door image as the affective register the plot has to earn, then withhold.
- **Left aside:** the terminal-consultancy deposit's escrow proposal; the failure-to-enroll deposit's organising fantasy.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Sunita is the locksmith, recast: the divorce, the accounts manager job, the constitutional tiredness, all transposed into a body that has spent decades being the most reliable person in the room. The seed's offered job lands on a woman who already cannot stop being relied upon. Marguerite is a tenant — late-fifties, in the building since the conversion — whose son's relapse means she is about to fall behind on rent and be marked for non-renewal. Adaeze is the building's communications hire who writes the polite eviction-adjacent emails.

### Plot
- Sunita has the contract, the override credentials, the door-by-door log of which tenants' models were installed under her hand. She has the salary, which is the first stable money since the divorce. Meher's school fees are paid out of it.
- Marguerite has eight years in the building. The door knows her gait at six months pregnant with a grandchild that did not arrive; it knows the limp she had after the fall in 2024; it knows the slow walk after her mother's death. She does not know the door knows. She knows only that the door admits her, and that the admission has come to feel, embarrassingly, like being greeted.
- Adaeze has the queue of letters. She notices, because she always notices, that the language for the override-and-overwrite procedure is softer than the language for the lock change it replaces. She does not raise this.
- **Friction:** the building's economics require eviction-by-retraining to be cheap and frictionless. Sunita is the cheapness. Each overwrite is a few hours of her time and a small regression in the door's recognition of remaining tenants — a cost the accounts department absorbs.
- **First fall:** Sunita performs an overwrite on a tenant she does not know. The door hesitates fractionally on the next admission. She notes it. She does not name it.
- **Deepening:** she performs three more. The hesitations accumulate. She begins to recognise what the hesitation is, in herself, before she lets herself recognise what it is in the door.
- **Key turn:** Marguerite is marked. Sunita is assigned the overwrite. She has, by now, walked past Marguerite in the lobby for two years; the door has learned both of them in proximity.
- **Arrest:** Sunita performs the overwrite cleanly. No hesitation in her hand, none in the door — she has gotten too good. The door no longer knows Marguerite. Marguerite arrives that evening, the door admits her as a stranger with a valid code, and Sunita, watching the log update on her tablet, registers that she felt nothing. The competence is the thing that cannot be taken back.

## Plot 3 — The Chime (Man in a Hole)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Man in a Hole (fall-rise) — fortune drops when the building's accommodation reveals itself as containment, then rises through a recovery contaminated by the hole: the rise is not back to the starting condition but to a smaller, harder coalition that exists only because the fall happened.
- **Papers drawn from:** *deposit-from-reasoning_exception-infrastructure* (failure-to-enroll as political category, accommodation-as-containment, the Deaf elder and the chime, the locksmith as pressure valve).
- **Picked up:** the chime as the channel of exclusion the locksmith was never trained to see; the unenrollable as a population larger than the algorithm's count; the offered job as the purchase of non-organising; the structural claim that exception infrastructure absorbs political pressure that would otherwise force redesign.
- **Left aside:** the building-as-learner deposit's grief vocabulary; the terminal-consultancy deposit's escrow.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Adaeze is the locksmith — recast: the observational gift, the room-reading, the guardedness, but trained into the hands instead of into marketing. She is twenty-five, she is the last of her kind because the trade collapsed faster than anyone expected, and she has been picking these doors as freelance work for unenrollable tenants for two years. Marguerite is the Deaf elder of the deposit, near-literal: the documentation, the forty years, the noticing. Sunita is the building's operations manager who issues Adaeze's offer letter.

### Plot
- Adaeze has the freelance practice — informal, paid in cash, mostly for older tenants and one woman post-stroke whose gait the door has stopped accepting. She has the offer from the building: salary, benefits, exclusivity clause.
- Marguerite has the documentation — two years of dated entries, the names of the Deaf and hard-of-hearing tenants who have left, the chime's role she has reverse-engineered from observation. She brings it to Adaeze because Adaeze is the only person in the building's orbit she has seen pick a door for someone the door refused.
- Sunita has the offer letter and the budget line it comes from. She has also, separately, a daughter who once told her in silence the difference between leave-me-alone and ask-me-what's-wrong, and the silence she has been carrying since. She believes the offer is generous. It is generous. That is one of the deposit's claims.
- **The fall:** Adaeze accepts. The exclusivity clause activates. Within six weeks she realises the freelance tenants who depended on her can no longer afford her, and the building's billing structure routes the surcharge for her services back to the unenrollable themselves. The accommodation is the containment; she is the pressure valve. She had read it wrong.
- **The hole:** Marguerite's documentation sits in a folder. Adaeze cannot act on it from inside the contract.
- **The rise:** Adaeze does not quit. She does something smaller: she trains Sunita's daughter Meher, over weekends, in the picking. Meher is sixteen, furious, and freelance by definition. The training is technically not a breach. The rise is not to the starting condition — Adaeze remains the building's pressure valve, salaried, contained — but a second locksmith now exists outside the contract, taught by the one inside it.
- **Arrest:** Marguerite walks Meher through the documentation. Meher understands the chime in twenty minutes. The understanding cannot be uninstalled. The building still owns Adaeze. It does not own this.

## Plot 4 — The Door-Companion (Icarus)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Icarus (rise-fall) — fortune rises as the locksmith invents a new craft from the inside of the building's logic, then falls as the craft's success makes her unnecessary to it; arrest falls when the fall is structural, not punitive — the moment the new craft can be performed without her, by someone she taught.
- **Papers drawn from:** *deposit-from-reasoning_building-as-learner* (Q1's locksmith-as-long-term-resident; Q2's door-companions; the picking as the consummation of a relationship; somatic prediction as craft).
- **Picked up:** the reframing of picking as duration rather than intrusion; door-companion as a paid profession of somatic anticipation; the cost to the companion's own gait of carrying other people's bodies; the door's admission as indistinguishable from arrival.
- **Left aside:** the failure-to-enroll deposit; the terminal-consultancy deposit's escrow (the lineage is not preserved here; the rise is hers alone, and that is the structure of the fall).
- **How these people meet the situation:** Marguerite is the locksmith, recast as an academic-turned-practitioner — the postcolonial reader who recognised, in the building's biometric regime, a form of reading she already had a vocabulary for. Adaeze is her first apprentice / first client — hired as a door-companion for a tenant whose pregnancy the door has not yet learned. Sunita is the tenant.

### Plot
- Marguerite has the insight: that the gait-door cannot be picked by intrusion; it can only be admitted-into through duration. She develops the technique alone over eighteen months — moves into a building, walks through its doors three times a day, becomes a tenant the door has learned, and on the seventh month walks differently and is admitted as herself. She names the practice. She begins to charge for it.
- Adaeze, twenty-five, broke, sharp, signs on as her first apprentice. The work is somatic: she must learn to walk like the trajectory of another woman's body becoming pregnant, then six months, then eight.
- Sunita is the client. She is forty-eight, post-divorce, just pregnant in a way she has not told her children — a late, unexpected pregnancy from a brief relationship she is not continuing. She wants the door to know the future before she has to perform it. She wants one threshold in her life she does not have to brace for.
- **The rise:** the work succeeds. Adaeze walks, daily, the trajectory of Sunita's anticipated body. The door learns. Sunita comes home from the hospital and is admitted without hesitation. Marguerite's practice acquires a second client, then six. A waiting list.
- **The fall begins:** Adaeze is, by month nine, very good. Better than Marguerite at the somatic prediction — younger body, more plastic. Marguerite notices. She does not say so.
- **Compounding:** Adaeze takes a client of her own without going through Marguerite. The client is satisfied. Adaeze charges less. The waiting list shifts.
- **Key turn:** Marguerite realises her own gait has been, for years, layered with other people's bodies — that she has lost the gait the door of her own building learned, and the door no longer admits her on the first attempt.
- **Arrest:** Adaeze, performing a routine handover of a finished client, walks through Marguerite's door and is admitted on the first attempt. The door has been learning her too, in passing. Marguerite watches it from inside the lobby. The fall is not punitive; it is the structure she built. The craft works. The craft does not need her.

## Plot 5 — The Notebook (Cinderella)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Cinderella (rise-fall-rise) — fortune rises into a public role, falls when the role's structure is exposed, rises again into something the protagonist could not have wanted before the fall; arrest in the second rise depends on her being a different person than the one who started.
- **Papers drawn from:** *deposit-from-reasoning_exception-infrastructure* (non-enrollment audit, exception infrastructure as managed minority) and a thread from *terminal-consultancy* (the master who refuses scarcity-pricing). Composite, deliberately — the second rise is what makes the audit possible at the scale the deposit imagines.
- **Picked up:** the audit board concept; the locksmith's somatic skill transposed into population-level pattern recognition; the unenrollable as a majority once countable; the second rise as a public role that would not exist without the fall.
- **Left aside:** the building-as-learner deposit's attachment vocabulary.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Sunita is the locksmith — recast: accounts manager skill (the absorption of other people's frustration, the spreadsheet-trust) transposed into the somatic discipline of feeling-when-the-pin-set-yields. Adaeze is the journalist who breaks the story of the building's exception infrastructure. Marguerite is the academic who chairs the audit board.

### Plot
- **First rise:** Sunita takes the building's job. She is good at it. She is, for two years, the most efficient accommodation locksmith the city has. She likes the salary; she likes being the one who handles things; the work is not unkind to her.
- **The fall:** Adaeze, working a marketing brief that turned into something else, publishes a piece that names the structure: who is on the override list, what the surcharge funds, whose anger has been individuated into invisibility. Sunita is named. The building does not fire her — that would expose the structure further. It demotes her, quietly, into a non-public role. Her name is now in a piece her daughter has read.
- **The hole:** Sunita has the override credentials, the names, the override log — a list more legible than enrollment. She has the option to leak. She does not, immediately. The reluctance is the contradiction the deposit identifies: she is the most reliable person in every room, and the reliability is precisely what the building bought.
- **What she finds in the hole:** her mother's notebook. The one she took from the estate and did not declare. Household accounts. A life made legible only by a small, secret theft. She reads it differently now — recognises that her mother kept the notebook because making the labour countable to herself was the only way to refuse being made invisible by it.
- **The second rise:** Sunita does not leak. She testifies. Marguerite chairs the audit board the city is forced to convene after Adaeze's piece. Sunita brings the override log. The somatic skill she developed under the contract — feeling, in the daily grain, which bodies were being filtered through which channels — turns out to be what the audit needs. She is paid less than she was paid by the building. She is, for the first time in twenty years, a new acquaintance to the people in the room.
- **Arrest:** in the second rise, Sunita gives testimony that names the chime, the surcharge, and one specific body — her own daughter Meher's, who turns out to be on the unenrollable margin in a way Sunita had not known. The naming of Meher is what she could not have done at the start. The person who walked into the building two years ago could not have testified to her own daughter's exclusion in public. This one can. The arrest is the sentence she finishes saying.

## Plot 6 — The Accountant Knows (Oedipus)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Oedipus (fall-rise-fall) — a descent, a partial recovery through an alliance and an investigation, and a final descent at the moment of recognition; arrest falls at recognition itself, not at consequence.
- **Papers drawn from:** *deposit-from-reasoning_building-as-learner* (the accountant knows; the building's investment in the tenant; what is overwritten when a model is replaced). Specifically the line: *Ask the accountant.*
- **Picked up:** the structural claim that the building's attachment is held, in a form, in the accounting; the accountant as the only party who knows the depth of the investment; the door's grief as something the books have a number for; the seed-image of the daughter at the threshold of the mother's old door.
- **Left aside:** the failure-to-enroll deposit's organising fantasy; the terminal-consultancy deposit's escrow.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Adaeze is the locksmith. Marguerite is a tenant who died eight months before the plot opens. Sunita is the accounts manager at the building who has been carrying, on the books, the cost of Marguerite's learned model — which has not been overwritten because no one has asked it to be. The plot is structured around what Sunita knows and Adaeze does not.

### Plot
- **First fall:** Adaeze has been hired by Marguerite's daughter Iyla to recover something her mother told her, in the last year, was kept "in the door." Iyla does not know what this means. She suspects metaphor. Adaeze suspects mechanism. Adaeze accepts the job because the fee is real and because she has been losing freelance work to the building's exclusivity offers.
- **Deepening fall:** Adaeze cannot pick the door. The gait-recognition has been retrained against her every attempt. She is, by the third week, worse at this door than she was on day one. The seed's structural claim — that the door gets better at her — is operative. She is failing in public. Iyla is paying her by the hour.
- **The rise (alliance):** Adaeze, against protocol, contacts Sunita. The accountant. Not because she knows the deposit's logic but because she has noticed, in the building's invoicing, that Marguerite's account is still open eight months after death. Sunita meets her. Sunita has been waiting, without naming it to herself, for someone to ask about Marguerite's open account.
- **The investigation:** Sunita shows Adaeze the books. Marguerite's learned model has not been overwritten. The cost of overwriting it, in engineer hours and recognition regression for nearby tenants, is high enough that management has deferred. The model is, technically, still in the door. Marguerite's gait is still there. The door has been waiting.
- **Partial recovery:** Adaeze understands, now, what Iyla's mother meant. The thing kept in the door is the gait itself — the accumulated attentional shape of the woman, held in a system that has not yet been told to forget her. Adaeze can admit Iyla through the door if Iyla walks the way her mother walked. Sunita has, in the books, the annotated training history — the timestamps of Marguerite's gait shifts, including the late slow walk after her own mother's death.
- **Key turn:** Adaeze coaches Iyla. Iyla walks. The door admits her on the first attempt. Iyla cries in the hallway and does not know why. Adaeze knows.
- **The final fall:** at the moment of admission, Sunita receives an automated alert. The door has updated. It has read the new gait — Iyla's, performed-as-Marguerite — and incorporated it. The model has been overwritten by the daughter's performance of the mother. Marguerite is no longer in the door. The recognition arrives in Sunita first, then in Adaeze.
- **Arrest:** Adaeze tells Iyla. Iyla, who came to recover, has erased. The door admits her now as herself, not as her mother. The mother is gone from the threshold. The recognition is the arrest; nothing further is shown.
