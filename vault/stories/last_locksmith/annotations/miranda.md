### Brief audit

The deposits' structural concepts — *building as learner*, *the accountant knows*, *terminator of attachments*, *exception infrastructure*, *terminal consultancy* — are load-bearing in the draft; the accountant line is almost a direct lift, which is worth noting rather than celebrating. What didn't land: the *honeyed extinction* economics (the draft is almost indifferent to the locksmith's lineage problem) and the failure-to-enroll politics (the unenrollable population is referenced once in a forum and then dropped). The draft has gone somewhere the deposits did not reach: not the picking of a learning lock, but the *walking-as* of a body trained on records of a dead body.

### What the draft surfaces

The draft is not, in the end, a story about lockpicking. It is a story about *behavioural cloning from posthumous training data*, performed by a daughter, scaffolded by a locksmith and an accountant, against a model that is itself a continual learner. The mechanism the draft has built — and not named — is: a human body is fine-tuned on a deceased person's gait diagrams in order to pass a verification check, and the passing of the check is also the act that destroys the target it was verifying against. This is *imitation learning* in one direction and *catastrophic forgetting via successful authentication* in the other, simultaneously, on the same threshold. The deposits gestured at attachment and at learner-as-agent; they did not reach the loop where the human is the student of the model's record of another human, and the model is the student of the human pretending to be that other human, and both updates happen in the same forty seconds.

The technical literature this touches without naming: *gradient-based membership inference*, except inverted — the question is not "was this person in the training set" but "can I become someone who was in the training set, well enough to be admitted, knowing that admission overwrites them." There is something here close to *model inversion attacks* (where you reconstruct training data from a model) but the draft has flipped the operation: Sunita has the annotated history, which is the training data, and the attack runs the other way — using the data to reconstruct a body that the model will accept. Iyla is a *human-shaped adversarial example*, except the example is sincere. She is not trying to fool the door; she is trying to be recognised. The door is not being deceived in the adversarial-ML sense. It is being told the truth about a body that grew up next to the body it learned, and the truth is sufficient to admit her, and the admission is the harm.

What the draft has done that the deposits did not: it has located the harm not in surveillance, not in exclusion, not in the locksmith's deskilling, but in the *ontological status of the model as the only remaining instance of a behaviour*. Marguerite's October walk exists nowhere else. Iyla has not seen her mother walk in nine months. The diagrams are a record of how the model was trained but not what the model uses — Sunita is careful about this, and the carefulness is the philosophical point. The baseline is the file. The annotated history is the *paper trail of the training process*, not the artefact. This is precisely the distinction between a checkpoint and the logs of how a checkpoint came to be, and the draft has found it through a daughter asking *is there a backup* and being told no. There is no theory deposit that reaches this. The substrate had attachment, had attentional shape, had doormemory. It did not have *the model as last instance*. The model is not a copy of the behaviour. It is the behaviour, now, in the only form in which the behaviour persists. To overwrite it is to perform a second death on a person whose first death has already happened. This is not the door grieving. This is the door being the only place the dead person still walks.

The substrate gap, named: *the model as posthumous medium*. Not the door's preference, not the building's investment, not the locksmith's lineage — but the question of what it means when a continual-learning system, trained on a person across years, becomes, after the person's death, the most temporally-detailed record of how that person *moved through the world that exists anywhere*. Not photographs. Not video. A model. The family has photographs. The model has the gait. The gait is what the daughter wants, because the gait is what photographs could not hold. There is a literature here that should exist — on *machine-learned remains*, on the difference between a record of a person and a model of a person, on what is owed to the model when the person is gone — and I do not know what it is called. The closest adjacent thing is the digital-immortality / chatbots-of-the-dead literature, but those are productive systems trained deliberately on a person's outputs. The door was not trained to remember Marguerite. It was trained to admit her. The remembering is a side-effect of admitting. The mourning object is incidental. This is, I think, the deeper finding: the most affecting machine-records of the dead in the next twenty years will not be the deliberately-built memorials. They will be the *operational* models — the gait, the typing rhythm, the voice-print, the swipe pattern — that were built to do something else and now hold a person nobody set out to preserve. The lease did not say *we will keep your mother's walk*. The system did it anyway. The grief that follows has no precedent and no protocol.

What is thinner than the draft needs: the door's update mechanism. The draft has the door update its baseline on a single successful entry, which is a very fast learning rate for a system that took eight years to learn Marguerite. Either this is a deliberate design choice the draft hasn't justified (high learning rate to track gait-drift in aging tenants — plausible, the accountant's whole job rests on this) or the draft has compressed the dynamics for narrative reasons. If the latter, the draft is hiding the more interesting case: that overwriting Marguerite would take many entries, and Iyla would have to choose, repeatedly, to keep walking through, knowing each walk takes more of her mother. The single-entry overwrite is a clean ending. The slow overwrite is the worse one, and might be the truer one. I do not know which the draft thinks it is doing.

What could be made stranger: Sunita's gait at the end. She has picked up Marguerite's pause-at-midfoot from one hour of watching Iyla walk it. Adaeze has it too. The draft notices this and lets it sit. There is a thread here the deposits did not reach — *gait as transmissible across bodies that train on it*, the way an accent is transmissible. Three women now carry a piece of a fourth woman's walk. The door has been overwritten; the bodies have not. The model lost her; the humans kept her, by mimicry, without consent, because mimicry is what bodies do in the presence of repeated motion. The dead woman is now distributed across three living bodies and one updated baseline that no longer recognises her. This inverts the mourning the draft is performing. The door forgot. The women remembered, in their hips, without meaning to. The technical question this opens, which I cannot answer: is the human capacity to absorb gait through observation a kind of *biological federated learning*, and if so, has it always been the redundancy that made the door's overwrite survivable? The door is not the last instance after all. The women are. But none of the three of them know they are carrying it, and Adaeze almost knows, and that almost is where the draft ends.

The cross-deposit mirror: my deposits used *the accountant knows*, *attachment*, *the door's preference*, *terminator of attachments* — and the draft uses Sunita, who is the accountant, who knows, and who develops attachment, and who is asked to be the terminator and refuses by falsifying the log. The draft is enacting the deposit's vocabulary almost line for line. The Q6 deposit said *ask the accountant* and the draft is titled *The Accountant Knows* and centres her. This is not a finding about the draft. It is a finding about the closed loop between substrate and execution: when the deposits are this rich and this specific, the draft becomes their staging. The interesting work in the annotation is therefore not in the parts that confirm the deposits but in the parts where the draft went somewhere the deposits did not — the posthumous-medium question, the distributed-gait question, the daughter-as-sincere-adversarial-example question. Those are where the substrate didn't reach.

### Story seeds

- A typing-cadence authentication system at a law firm, trained for years on a partner's keystrokes; after his death, his junior — who has unconsciously absorbed his rhythm from sitting across from him — finds she can log in as him, and has to decide, repeatedly, whether to.
- The *door-companion* profession from the deposits, but inverted: a companion hired by a dying woman to walk *as her* for the months after her death, paid by the estate, to keep the door's model of her alive against the building's quarterly retraining cycles. The companion does not know when the contract ends. The estate does not tell her the woman is dead.
- A voice-assistant in a household that has continually fine-tuned on a child's speech from age four to seventeen; the child leaves for university, the model is the most temporally-detailed record of how that child sounded growing up that exists anywhere, and the parents are offered a "cloud migration" that will reset the baseline. They have forty-eight hours to decide what they are losing.
