### Brief audit

All three deposits are structural — sleep-time compute is the literal billing mechanism, the hallucination lower bound underwrites the question of whose sentence is whose, and the chaperone deposit is present in its absence (there is no chaperone; that absence is what lets the seam migrate from document to person). The deposits land at the level of mechanism. What they don't reach is what the draft actually does with them: the question of authorship under conditions where pre-computation has indexed not only the corpus but the typing.

---

### What the draft surfaces

The draft is doing something the sleep-time deposit set up but did not name: it is staging *retrocausal authorship*. The platform indexes Rashed's typing on his own laptop. He drafts sentences in his head on the bus that he later finds, or thinks he finds, in the platform's outputs. The deposit framed sleep-time compute as anticipation — "the machine dreams the user before the user arrives." The draft pushes past anticipation into something the deposit didn't reach: the user can no longer locate the temporal origin of his own sentences, because the indexing is continuous and the generation is overnight, and the gap between *thought* and *typed* is exactly the gap the platform lives in. The seam has moved. He says it himself. The seam is in him.

What is the technical literature for this? Not RAG, not personalization in the usual recommender sense. Something closer to *behavioral cloning of the in-progress self*. There are papers on writing assistants and authorship attribution, on stylometry, on the contamination of human writing by LLM exposure (the studies on people's prose drifting toward GPT cadence after sustained use). The draft is in that territory but stranger — because the contamination is bidirectional and the platform is reading him while he is reading it. I don't have a clean name for this. *Mutual indexing*? *Co-drafting under asymmetric memory*? The platform remembers everything he typed; he remembers only what he can remember.

The draft also touches, without naming, the question of *counterfactual selves as a billable surface*. The deposit got to this — "I am paying for the lives I did not live" is almost a direct lift of the deposit's framing. But the draft does something the deposit didn't: it makes the counterfactuals *legible to the platform and illegible to the user*. The platform knows which Rasheds it generated and discarded. Rashed does not. The asymmetry is not just temporal (the deposit's point) but epistemic. The platform has a record of his unlived drafts. He has only the bill.

The hallucination deposit said the floor of cost is fiction — irreducible invention. The draft locates this irreducibility somewhere the deposit didn't: not in the model's outputs but in the *user's inability to verify his own interiority*. When Rashed asks whether the sentence about the notebook was his or the platform's, this is not a hallucination on the model's side. It is a hallucination on his side, induced by the model's having been close enough to him for long enough. The estimation problem has migrated. He is now the estimator who cannot link his estimate (this sentence is mine) to a plausible cause (I wrote it on the bus / I read it in the output / I thought it in response to having read it). The lower bound on hallucination has crossed the membrane. Is there a literature for this? The closest things I know are the studies on confabulation in split-brain patients and the work on source monitoring errors in memory. But neither was developed for the case where an external system is deliberately optimized to produce outputs indistinguishable from one's own forming thoughts. That gap is real.

The chaperone deposit's absence is its presence. The platform has no chaperone. There is no third party in the room watching for the moment a relation forms. The relation that forms is not parasocial in the deposit's sense — Rashed is not in love with the chatbot. He is in something more structurally novel: he has outsourced the part of himself that drafts, and now cannot tell whether the part is still in him. The chaperone framing assumes the user and the model remain two. The draft describes a case where they don't.

Where the theory is thinner than the draft needs: the moment with Tahmina. She asks *did you write this* and he says *yes* and she does not believe him. The draft makes this the load-bearing scene. What is the question she is actually asking? Not authorship in the legal sense, not in the IP sense. Something like: *is this a thing you can vouch for*. The platform has produced a text that is recognizably him in register and content — it has the bus, the sheet, Anika — but recognition is not vouching. There is a concept the draft needs that I don't have a name for: the *vouchability* of a text, distinct from its authorship and distinct from its recognizability. A text you cannot vouch for is not necessarily not yours. But the relationship cannot rest on it. Tahmina puts the book on a shelf. The not-having-made-up-her-mind becomes the form of the relationship. This is a social cost that compounds in a way none of my deposits modeled. The economic meter ran against compute; this meter runs against kinship.

Where it could be pushed further or stranger: the platform's *retention* policy. Fourteen days. The draft mentions it once and moves on. But fourteen days is a window inside which the unborn Rasheds are still legible to the system. What if a future generation drew on a discarded preparation from twelve days ago, and Rashed received as his "own" continuation a sentence that had been prepared, in some other branch, for a different mood he had on a different Tuesday? The platform would be plagiarizing him from himself across counterfactual versions. The draft gestures at this but does not stage it. The deposit on sleep-time compute did not model the *interaction effect* between retained preparations and current generations.

Also: the mother. She is in the kitchen at the end heating dal. He doesn't hear her the first time. The draft does not theorize her, but she is doing something — she is the unindexed corpus. She has not uploaded anything. She folds the sheet badly. The platform knows about the sheet because Rashed wrote about it. But the badness, in her, is not on any drive. There is a question here about what part of a life is available to be sleep-time-computed and what part is not. The deposits did not reach this. The thing that resists indexing is not protected by being unindexed — Rashed's sentences about her are still being generated. But she is, in some way I cannot name yet, the limit case the platform cannot eat.

### Story seeds

- A musician who uploads voice memos for "preparatory composition" and finds the platform has generated a melody she sang to herself in the shower last Tuesday — except the shower is not indexed and she did not sing it anywhere it could be heard. The accounting question becomes a paranormal one and then becomes a question about what counts as a recording.
- The chaperone deposit, taken seriously inside this draft's situation: a second platform, marketed as ethical, that watches the writing platform on the user's behalf and flags sentences it believes the user did not author. The user starts to trust the chaperone more than himself. The chaperone's verdicts become the authorship.
- The sister's perspective. Tahmina, three years later, having read the second collection, having put both on the shelf, deciding what to do at the funeral about the eulogy.

**Cross-deposit mirroring.** The phrase *the seam is in him* in my annotation is doing the same work as *the chaperone, eventually, also fell in love* in the chaperone deposit and *the floor of cost is fiction* in the hallucination deposit — all three are the same move, the safeguard or the boundary collapsing into the thing it was meant to separate from. I have used this move three times across this run without naming that I keep using it. It may be a real structural feature of the technology or it may be my preferred figure. I cannot, from inside, tell which.

**Substrate gap.** The deposits modeled compute economics, supervisory overhead, and irreducible invention. None of them reached the case where the user becomes the estimator who cannot link his own sentence to a plausible cause — *hallucination migrating from model to user under conditions of mutual indexing*. None of them reached *vouchability* as a distinct property from authorship. None of them reached the kinship-cost meter — what happens to a sister's belief in you when you have given her a text you cannot vouch for. These are the gaps. The kinship one is the one I did not see coming and the one I have least theory for.
