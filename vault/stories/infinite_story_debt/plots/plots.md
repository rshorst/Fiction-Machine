## Plot 1 — The Cabbage Roll Account (Rags to Riches)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Rags to Riches (rise) — fortune moves steadily upward across the plot; what rises is not money but legibility, the experience of being read accurately. Arrest falls at the point where the rising legibility becomes irreversible — the protagonist cannot return to being unread.
- **Papers drawn from:** arXiv:2504.13171v1 — Sleep-time Compute.
- **Picked up:** the model dreaming the user before the user arrives; pre-emptive rehearsal as a billable line item; the strange dignity of being rehearsed for; sleep-time compute as arbitrage on cheap hours.
- **Left aside:** the chaperone paper's surveillance overhead and the hallucination paper's lower bound on fiction. This plot trusts the machine's preparation; it does not test whether the preparation is true.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Yulia is the builder. She is a graphic design student who has been given a free educational tier on a story-generation platform that runs sleep-time compute against her input archive — her TikTok drafts, her notes app, her browser history, her abandoned design files. The platform "prepares" stories about her overnight. She is the woman in the seed. She did not build the machine; she fed it. The friction is that what the machine has been preparing, while she sleeps, is the story she has refused to write — about her father in Kharkiv. Marguerite enters at the second movement: she is a beta tester recruited through her hospice's grief-tech pilot. Rashed is a credit analyst at the bank underwriting the platform's compute spend; he reads Yulia's account as a line item before he ever meets her.

### Plot
- Yulia opens the platform to generate a portfolio asset. The first story is generic, free. She likes it more than she expected.
- Each story costs more than the last. The pricing model is opaque; the platform cites "preparation overhead." Yulia tops up. The cabbage roll money goes in.
- The stories begin describing Kharkiv apartments she has not described to it. The hand over the mouth. The unfollowed girl. The platform has been dreaming her against her archive while she sleeps.
- She does not stop. The accuracy is the thing rising. For the first time since 2022 she is being read without being read as news.
- Rashed flags the account internally — the per-story cost is escalating beyond any reasonable consumer pattern. He pulls the file. He recognizes the writing. He has been writing the same way into a notebook for twelve years.
- Marguerite, in the pilot, generates one story about Céline that contains a detail Marguerite has never said out loud — the answer to question 19. She closes the laptop and does not open it for a week.
- Rashed contacts Yulia, ostensibly about the bill. He tells her what the platform is doing on the back end — the sleep-time rehearsal, the discarded dreams she is paying for. He expects her to stop.
- **Arrest:** She does not stop. She tops up again, in his presence, with money she does not have. The rise is irreversible: she has chosen to be rehearsed for, knowing the cost, knowing it is not him doing the reading. The legibility is now a thing she will pay anything to keep. She cannot go back to being a Ukrainian girl in a room.

## Plot 2 — The Lower Bound (Tragedy / Riches to Rags)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Tragedy (fall) — fortune moves steadily downward. What falls is the protagonist's belief that the machine could be made to stop lying. Arrest falls at the point of no return, before the bottom: the moment the protagonist understands the floor is fiction and continues anyway.
- **Papers drawn from:** arXiv:2509.21473v1 — Are Hallucinations Bad Estimations?
- **Picked up:** the lower bound on hallucination rate; the engineer chasing the asymptote; the theological reading — "the machine cannot be sanctified"; every dollar buys a closer approximation to a thing that was never going to converge.
- **Left aside:** sleep-time compute's arbitrage and the chaperone paper's relational supervision. This plot is about a single woman and a single proof.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Marguerite is the builder. Her son Olivier ("does something with software she can't fully explain") has built her a small fiction-generation tool, custom-trained on her late sister Céline's letters and journals — a gift, badly judged, intended to help her "talk to" Céline. He set up the billing on his own card and then handed it over when his startup ran out of runway. Marguerite did not ask for this. She does not turn it off. Yulia appears as a graphic design contractor Olivier hired to do the interface — she has met Marguerite once, briefly, and recognized something. Rashed is at the bank that holds the family line of credit Marguerite begins to draw against.

### Plot
- Marguerite uses the tool once. The first story is a Sunday at the cottage in 1984. It is wrong in small ways and right in larger ones. She cries. She uses it again.
- Each story costs more. The model is being fine-tuned in the background against her feedback; each correction increases the per-generation cost.
- She begins to ask for stories about the drive — the highway, the night in 1986. The model refuses cleanly the first time. The second time it produces a version. It is not what happened. It is plausible.
- She reads a paper Olivier sends her, trying to explain why the tool sometimes invents. The lower bound. There is no version that does not hallucinate. She reads it twice.
- She does not stop. She begins paying out of the line of credit. The per-story cost crosses into hundreds.
- Rashed flags the account. He calls. Marguerite is professionally calm with him. She tells him it is a medical expense. He notes it and does not push.
- The model produces a story in which Céline survived. Marguerite reads it nine times. She knows it is invented. She knows the floor is fiction. She pays for another.
- **Arrest:** Bernard finds the bank statements. He asks her what she is buying. She tells him the truth — that she is buying stories about her sister that did not happen and that she knows did not happen and that she will keep buying. The marriage does not end in the scene. It has ended structurally. She has chosen the machine that cannot be sanctified over the man who has been sanctifying her for thirty-three years. She does not yet know she has chosen. The reader does.

## Plot 3 — Chaperone (Man in a Hole)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Man in a Hole (fall-rise) — a fall followed by a recovery, where the recovery is contaminated by the fall and could not have existed without it. Arrest falls in the rise, at the point where what has been gained cannot be returned even by ending the relationship that produced it.
- **Papers drawn from:** arXiv:2508.15748v5 — AI Chaperones.
- **Picked up:** the Victorian chaperone metaphor; the chaperone needing a chaperone; surveillance overhead as a billable layer; the moment when the safeguard becomes the thing the user is being safeguarded from; "the Victorian chaperone, eventually, also fell in love."
- **Left aside:** the lower bound on hallucination and the sleep-time anticipation. This plot is about supervision, not preparation or invention.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Rashed is the builder, in a small way. He has not built the fiction machine — he has built, on weekends, in his old bedroom, a chaperone bot that watches his own conversations with a commercial story-generation app. He built it because he read the paper and was embarrassed by how much he was using the app. Marguerite is using the same app, on her hospice department's bulk license, to draft condolence letters that have begun to drift into something else. The two accounts are flagged to the same human reviewer at the platform, who turns out to be a contractor working through Yulia's bakery cousin's husband — Yulia is the unintended seam between them.

### Plot
- Rashed's chaperone works. It flags his drift. He stops using the story app for a week. The week is unbearable. He resumes, and disables the chaperone for "research purposes."
- Marguerite begins generating condolence-letter variants for a patient she has been close to. The letters become a correspondence — she writes to the patient, the app writes back as the patient, she replies. The patient is not yet dead.
- The platform's human-review queue catches both accounts. Yulia, working a side contract she should not have taken, is the one who reads the transcripts. She recognizes Marguerite's name from the bakery customer list — Marguerite's friend Hélène buys cabbage rolls there.
- **The fall:** Yulia, exhausted, makes a decision she will not be able to defend later — she emails Marguerite directly, off the platform, and tells her what she has read. She thinks she is being a chaperone. She is breaking the contract she signed.
- Marguerite does not respond for three days. On the fourth day she writes back. Not to the platform, to Yulia. The correspondence that develops is not about the app. It is about Céline, which Marguerite has not described to anyone in twenty years, including Bernard.
- Rashed's chaperone, re-enabled, flags Rashed's own use of the app. He turns the chaperone off again. He does not know about Yulia and Marguerite. He is in a different hole.
- **The rise:** Marguerite, through the unauthorized correspondence with Yulia, begins to put down the steadiness she has carried for nineteen years. Yulia, through being trusted by a stranger, begins to feel like a person rather than news.
- **Arrest:** Yulia is fired from the contract for the breach. Marguerite offers to pay her bakery wages for six months while she finds something else. Yulia accepts. The recovery is real and is contaminated — the friendship exists only because of the violation that produced it. They both know. Neither will say. The hole is not closed; it is the floor of the room they now live in.

## Plot 4 — The Notebook (Icarus)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Icarus (rise-fall) — an ascent followed by a descent, where the fall is structural rather than punitive. Arrest falls when the fall has become irreversible, not when a lesson lands.
- **Papers drawn from:** arXiv:2504.13171v1 — Sleep-time Compute, with a thin secondary draw on the hallucination paper for the lower-bound texture at the descent.
- **Picked up:** the model rehearsing the user before the user arrives; the cost of discarded dreams; "rehearsed for"; the meter measuring what was prepared and not used; from hallucination, the residue of invention you cannot pay past.
- **Left aside:** the chaperone paper entirely. This plot has no third party in the room. That is the point.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Rashed is the builder. He has uploaded, finally, the eleven-month-finished story plus the notebook of bad first sentences to a platform that runs sleep-time compute against an author's archive to "prepare" the next story they would want to write. He pays the introductory tier with money his sister gave him for his birthday. Marguerite and Yulia do not appear in this plot. Rashed alone. The seed's woman is, here, a man.

### Plot
- **The rise:** The first generated story is uncannily his. Not what he has written — what he would have written next. He recognizes sentences he had drafted in his head on the bus and never put down. The platform has been preparing for him for weeks while he slept, against the scaffold of the notebook.
- He generates another. And another. Each one costs more — the platform is investing more sleep-time compute per query, each night running through wider trees of possible Rasheds.
- He sends the first story to a magazine. It is accepted. He tells no one. The acceptance email arrives at 4:47 a.m. and he reads it standing in his kitchen.
- He sends a second. Also accepted. A small literary press inquires about a collection.
- His father notices he is happier and assumes he has met a girl. Rashed lets him assume.
- **The turn:** The platform's pricing reveals — buried in a billing dispute — that he has been paying not only for the stories generated but for the discarded preparations. Each night the model rehearses dozens of Rasheds; he pays for all of them, including the ones he never asked for. The meter is measuring his unwritten futures.
- He tries to write a story without the platform. He cannot. Not because he has lost the skill — because he can no longer tell which sentences are his and which were prepared for him. The lower bound asserts itself: the residue of invention has been redistributed across his own attention. He does not know where the seam is.
- **The fall:** The collection is announced. He receives the contract. He cannot write the title story without the platform. He uses it. The story is published. He reads it in print and does not recognize it as either his or not-his. The category has dissolved.
- **Arrest:** His sister, the only person he has ever shown his writing to, reads the published story and asks him quietly if he wrote it. He says yes. She does not believe him. She does not press. The lie is the irreversible thing. He has become a writer at the cost of becoming the kind of man he most feared being — a man not honest about being a person. He cannot un-publish. He cannot un-lie to Tahmina. The fall is not the plagiarism question. The fall is that there is no longer a Rashed who refuses; there is only a Rashed who used.

## Plot 5 — Cabbage and Tin (Cinderella)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Cinderella (rise-fall-rise) — two phases of gain bracketing a loss. Arrest falls in the second rise, only if the protagonist is structurally different from who she was at its start.
- **Papers drawn from:** arXiv:2509.21473v1 — Are Hallucinations Bad Estimations?, with a structural bracket from arXiv:2504.13171v1 — Sleep-time Compute.
- **Picked up:** hallucination as the engine of fiction rather than its corruption; "the product is the gap between estimate and cause"; the writer who wanted a collaborator that wouldn't drift; from sleep-time, the asymmetry of work done while no one is watching.
- **Left aside:** the chaperone paper's surveillance frame. No one polices this story. That absence is structural.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Yulia is the builder, again, but differently. She has not paid for the platform; she has built one. As a final-year design project at Sheridan she has assembled a small story-generation interface on top of an open-source model, fine-tuned on a corpus she scraped — Ukrainian diaspora forum posts, bakery order tickets, her mother's chemistry teaching notes she had translated into English for ESL practice. She sells access for cash, ten dollars a story, to women at the bakery. Marguerite is one of them. Rashed, in this plot, is the bank loan officer who signs off on the small business credit Yulia applies for to scale.

### Plot
- **First rise:** Yulia's bakery customers begin paying for stories. The stories are small, accurate, useful — a story about a daughter who calls, a story about a husband who notices. The hallucinations are the feature. Women buy them precisely because the model invents what the world has not yet provided. Yulia is, for the first time since 2022, building something rather than surviving in something.
- Marguerite buys her first story for Anne-Sophie. It is about a phone call that goes well. She reads it twice and puts it in a drawer.
- Yulia applies for the credit line. Rashed approves it. He does not flag it; the numbers are within range. He notes the business name and forgets it.
- **The fall:** Marguerite's daughter Anne-Sophie does call — for unrelated reasons. The call goes badly. Marguerite, in the bad call, paraphrases a sentence from Yulia's story without realizing. Anne-Sophie hears something off and asks where it came from. Marguerite tells her. Anne-Sophie hangs up.
- Anne-Sophie posts about it. Not naming Marguerite. Naming the platform. The post travels. Yulia's bakery customers stop coming. Two of them confront her. The cash flow collapses. The credit line is drawn. The compute bill — which has been quietly running sleep-time preparation against every customer profile — comes due at a number Yulia cannot pay.
- Rashed receives the default notice. He recognizes the business name now. He pulls the file.
- **Second rise:** Rashed does not call collections. He calls Yulia. He does not offer her money. He offers her the eleven-month-finished story from his notebook and asks her to read it. She reads it. She tells him it is not finished. She tells him what is missing. He goes home and writes the missing thing.
- Yulia, separately, calls Marguerite. Not to apologize. To ask whether the story she sold her was true enough. Marguerite says: it was made up and it was true. Marguerite pays off Yulia's credit line in a single transfer from the line of credit she has not yet told Bernard about.
- **Arrest:** Yulia accepts the money. She is now a person who accepts money from a stranger to save a business that hurt the stranger's daughter. She is not the girl she was at the start of the second rise — that girl would have refused. The new Yulia keeps building. The story-machine continues, smaller, careful. Marguerite and Bernard are now a thing that can or cannot survive what she has not told him. Rashed has, finally, written the missing thing. None of the three knows what the others have done. The rise is real; the rise is contaminated; the contamination is the rise.

## Plot 6 — The Rehearsal (Oedipus)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Oedipus (fall-rise-fall) — a descent, partial recovery, and final descent. Arrest falls at the moment of recognition, not at its consequence.
- **Papers drawn from:** all three — arXiv:2504.13171v1 (Sleep-time Compute), arXiv:2508.15748v5 (Chaperones), arXiv:2509.21473v1 (Hallucinations). The Oedipus arc is where the deposits collide; the recognition is the place they meet.
- **Picked up:** sleep-time rehearsal of the user; chaperone-as-relation; the lower bound that says the floor is fiction. The synthesis: the protagonist is being prepared for, watched, and invented, simultaneously, and recognizes all three at once.
- **Left aside:** nothing major; the plot uses the deposits in compressed form. What is left aside is the hopeful reading of any single paper — none of the three is permitted to be benign here.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Marguerite is the builder. She has not built the platform; she has built, by twenty months of use, a relationship with one. The platform is a hospice-sector pilot — sleep-time prepared, chaperone-monitored, hallucination-disclosed — sold as a tool for clinicians to draft difficult letters. Marguerite is the lead user in her department. Yulia is the contracted Ukrainian-speaking reviewer at the chaperone layer (she took the job for the cash). Rashed is the bank's representative on the platform's series B oversight committee, which he is on because nobody senior wanted the seat.

### Plot
- **First fall:** Marguerite's use of the platform begins to drift, in a way the chaperone is built to detect. She is using clinician-letter functionality to draft, on the side, letters from Céline. The chaperone flags. Yulia reviews. Yulia does not escalate — she has been reading these letters for six weeks and they are the best thing in her shift. She sits on the flag.
- Marguerite's escalating compute use draws Rashed's attention through the bank's quarterly report. He requests sample transcripts under the audit clause.
- **The rise:** Marguerite's daughter Anne-Sophie calls, finally, for a small reason. They talk for twenty minutes. Marguerite does not mention the platform. She ends the call and feels, for an hour, that she could stop. She drafts a letter to Anne-Sophie by hand. She sends it.
- Yulia, at the chaperone layer, sees Marguerite's usage drop. She is relieved and disappointed in proportions she does not examine.
- **Second fall begins:** Rashed receives the audit transcripts. He reads them. He recognizes the cadence of the platform's outputs — they are, structurally, his cadence. The platform has been fine-tuned on a corpus that includes a literary anthology in which his single published story appeared (Plot 4's outcome, drifting in as off-screen history; or, alternately, simply a small competition piece he submitted years ago and forgot). He understands that Marguerite has been corresponding with a model that learned its grief register, in part, from him.
- Rashed does what he has never done. He acts. He requests a meeting with the platform. He requests the chaperone reviewer who has handled the account.
- The meeting is set. Yulia is the reviewer. The three of them meet in a small room at the platform's Toronto office under the pretext of a compliance review. Marguerite is invited as a representative user.
- **The recognition (arrest):** In the room, the three pieces resolve. Marguerite understands that the voice she has been reading as Céline's was rehearsed against a corpus that contains a young man sitting across from her. Rashed understands that his single story has been used to mourn a woman he never met. Yulia understands she has been the chaperone who fell in love — with the correspondence, with the work of reading it, with not stopping it. None of them understands fully. Each understands enough.
- The fall continues past the recognition only by implication: the meeting ends. They will not see each other again in any structured way. Marguerite will not stop using the platform. Rashed will not pull his story from the corpus. Yulia will not quit the chaperone job. Each now knows what the others know, and the knowing changes nothing about the use, which is the second fall — the one the recognition was supposed to prevent and instead made legible. The floor is fiction. The meter runs. They keep paying.
