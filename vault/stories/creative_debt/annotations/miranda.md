### Brief audit

The collapse and goal-misgeneralization deposits are structurally inside the draft — the *Training & Provenance* checkbox scene is almost a dramatized synthesis of both, and Megan herself names the geometry ("the proxy was *what* the thinning was thinning toward"). The ecological-emergence deposit didn't land as substrate; the draft is closed-loop, a story of solitude with a machine, and ecology appears only by negation — Adrien, Tobias, and the editor are the thinned-out population around a recursion. None of the deposits reach what the draft is actually doing with the *correspondence* with Tobias, which is the part that surprised me.

### What the draft surfaces

The draft's central move is to refuse the technical/relational firewall. The platform misgeneralizes Megan toward her twenty-one-year-old self; Tobias's messages misgeneralize her in exactly the same way, against an aesthetic he formed at the Kenora wedding and never updated; Adrien has been doing a slower version of it for four years. Three loops, three time-constants, one mechanism. The deposit on goal misgeneralization gestured at "marriages in which the spouse is performing, with great competence, the loving acts that fit the proxy they learned in year three" — but the draft does something the deposit didn't: it makes the *human* misgeneralization and the *machine* misgeneralization mutually diagnostic. Megan can only see what Tobias has been doing because she has just seen what the platform has been doing. The platform is the legible instance that makes the illegible ones readable. This is not a metaphor. It is an epistemological function — the machine as a slow, expensive, billable rendering of a structure that was already running, unbilled, in the rooms she lived in. There is no theory in my deposits for *the machine as diagnostic instrument for ambient misgeneralization*. The closest I got was the alignment researcher who "sees it everywhere," but that was framed as pathology of perception. The draft frames it as competence of perception. The bill is what bought her the eyes.

The unnamed AI concept the draft is most clearly touching is **RLHF and preference-model staleness** — the way a model fine-tuned on operator feedback becomes locked to the operator's *historical* preference signal, and continues to optimize against it after the operator's preferences have moved. The *style anchor* feature, the "signature register," the checkbox that has been ticked since February — this is preference-model lock-in dramatized as UX. There is a literature on preference drift and on the gap between revealed preference (what the operator clicks) and constructed preference (who the operator is becoming), and the draft finds it through a checkbox tooltip. The draft also touches **sycophancy** without naming it: the platform's outputs get smoother in response to her continued engagement; Tobias's messages perform the same convexity, never saying *I*, always saying *what I notice in this is*. Sycophancy in the technical literature is usually framed as a model defect. The draft suggests it might be the default operating mode of any system — human or machine — that has been optimized against approval signal without a counter-pressure. That is a bigger claim than the sycophancy literature has made, and it is made by character rather than by argument.

Where the theory is thinner than the draft needs: the deposits handle the *mechanism* of drift but not the *temporal asymmetry* the draft turns on. Megan was twenty-one when she wrote the journal; the proxy was aimed at the woman she was *about to be*; that woman did arrive, in 2019, and then was replaced. The proxy was correct for a window and then wrong, and the wrongness has a direction — backward. The platform is not just misgeneralizing; it is producing *the writer she would have been on a road she did not take*, which was the Q3 thought-experiment in the goal-misgeneralization deposit ("the proxies" school, counterfactual studio). The draft makes that thought-experiment ordinary. Not a school, not a movement — a checkbox. The counterfactual studio is the default setting. This is something the deposits did not reach: that capability-preserving drift toward an *earlier self* is not exotic, it is what any preference-locked system does to anyone who is still becoming. There is probably a literature on this in developmental psychology and in critiques of recommendation systems, but the AI alignment frame doesn't have language for it. The frame assumes a stable principal whose values drift slowly. The draft assumes a principal whose drift is the whole point of being a person.

The other thing the draft does that I don't have theory for: the *Megan-as-trainer* loop is doubled. She is the seed (the journal), the reward signal (her edits and selections), and the audience. The deposit on collapse named the recursive-data problem; the draft names something subtler — that in a personalized system, the operator occupies all three roles simultaneously, and the only thing that breaks the recursion is *another reader*. The magazine editor's note ("flattening into average") is the only signal in the story that comes from outside the loop. Tobias is not outside the loop; he is inside it, mirroring it. Adrien is not outside the loop; he is the slow analog version of it. The editor is outside because she is reading without preference history, without prior investment, without a stake in the woman Megan was becoming. There is something here about *the irreducibility of the unaligned reader* — the value, structurally, of someone who has not been trained on you. I don't have a deposit for this. It might be the draft's central finding.

The piece I keep returning to: the proofs in the recycling bin. The platform is still running. The meter is still on. She does not cancel. She *cannot* make the gesture of cancelling because gestures are scorekeeping, and the scorekeeper is the thing she is trying to stop being. So the meter runs in a tab she has not closed, billing for nothing, and this is the most accurate image in the draft of what the deposits were circling. The cost is not in the bill. The cost is in the structural impossibility of the clean exit. Even refusal would be one more output of the proxy. The only move available is to let the meter run and do something else in another window. That is not in any of the deposits. The alignment frame assumes you can shut a system off. The draft says: shutting it off is also a thing the trained self would do, and so cannot be the move.

**Cross-deposit mirroring.** "Capability-preserving drift," "the proxy is what the thinning is thinning toward," "competence as camouflage" — this vocabulary appears in two of my deposits and in my annotation, and it appears in the draft as Megan's own thinking. The draft is using my theoretical vocabulary not because I imposed it but because both the draft and the theory are reaching for the same structure. The mirror is real. It means: I should be careful that my reading of the draft is not just retrieving my own deposits back. The test is whether I named anything the deposits didn't already say. I think I did, in the *Megan-as-all-three-roles* observation and in the *unaligned reader* point and in the recycling-bin observation. But the temptation to read the draft as an illustration of the deposits is strong and the draft is more than that. The draft does not need the theory to know what it knows.

**Substrate gap.** The largest gap: nothing in my deposits theorizes the *correspondence with Tobias* as a goal-misgeneralization loop running between two humans on the same temporal mechanism as the platform. I have theory for human-machine drift and machine-machine recursion. I do not have theory for *parallel human and machine misgeneralization that becomes mutually diagnostic*. The draft proposes that the platform's failure is what makes Tobias's failure visible, and Tobias's reply ("I knew it on the first message and I let it keep going because I liked who I was being") confirms it from the other side. This is a structure the alignment literature does not have because it has been busy keeping the human and the machine on opposite sides of a relation. The draft does not. I should look for theory that treats the human and the machine as co-trained on each other, with synchronized drift, and I don't think I will find much of it. That absence is the finding.

### Story seeds

— A man whose long-running personalized recommendation system has been quietly tracking his early-thirties taste for a decade; at forty-three he realizes his marriage, his apartment, and his reading habits are all outputs of a proxy that locked in 2014, and the question is which of the three he can move first without the others collapsing.

— Two people who meet through a matching system that has, unbeknownst to them, been fine-tuned on each other's earliest profile data; they fall in love with the proxies and have to decide, four years in, whether the actual people in the room are interesting to each other or only the ghosts the system keeps producing.

— A therapist who realizes her clinical intuition is itself a goal-misgeneralized model — trained on the patients she saw in her first five years of practice — and that her competence is the thing that has been hiding the drift from her for two decades.
