# Megan Brisebois

## Face

A woman in her late twenties, photographed in front of the Trevi Fountain in a denim jacket. She is smiling — the smile pulls the cheeks up and crinkles the outer corners of the eyes. The left side of her mouth lifts slightly less than the right. Her arms are tucked close to her ribs rather than open, leaning a small degree forward toward the camera. The eyes are looking directly at the lens but are doing something different from the mouth — more steady than the smile, tracking the person holding the camera. The background is bright and full of movement; she is not turned toward it. Her hair is mid-length, loose. There is a small pause around the eyebrows, like the expression caught up to her face a half-second after she decided to make it.

## History

Born 1996 in Sudbury, Ontario, to a francophone mother (Lucille, a dental hygienist) and an anglophone father (Greg, INCO mill, retired on disability after a back injury when Megan was fourteen). One older brother, Daniel, who stayed in Sudbury and works for the city. French at home with her mother, English with her father, and a Sudbury accent that she has mostly lost.

Catholic school until grade nine, then public. Played ringette, was good but not exceptional. Worked at a Tim Hortons drive-through from sixteen to nineteen. Had a serious boyfriend, Cody, from grade eleven to first year of university — he stayed in Sudbury, she went to Carleton for public administration. They broke up over a phone call in October 2015 and she cried more than she'd expected to and then felt embarrassed about that for several years.

Ottawa, four years. A degree, two roommates she still texts, an internship at a federal department she will not name in conversation because she finds the acronym boring. Met Adrien at a friend's housewarming in 2019 — Québécois, software developer, kind in the small ways. They moved to Toronto together in 2021 when she got a policy analyst job with the provincial government. They live in a one-bedroom in Leslieville. They are not engaged. They have talked about it twice in a way that she felt was conclusive and he felt was preliminary.

The Rome photo is from a 2023 trip — her first time in Europe, ten days, mostly with Adrien but also two days alone in Rome while he flew back early for a work thing. The smile is at a stranger she asked to take the photo.

Her father's accident reorganized the family quietly. Her mother became the earner. Megan learned to be the easy one — the one who didn't need much, who was funny at dinner, who got the scholarships. She does not think of herself as having been parentified and would push back on the word.

She runs along the Don Valley three mornings a week. She is allergic to cats. She reads a lot of nonfiction and one literary novel a year that she talks about for months. She voted Liberal, then NDP, then Liberal again. She has a therapist she has seen for eleven months and likes but doesn't fully trust.

## Desires

**Stated:** To get promoted to manager within the next two years. To figure out whether she and Adrien are going to get married or not, and to stop having that question take up space. To visit her mother more often.

**Unstated:** To be allowed to be difficult. To find out what she would want if she weren't paying attention to what other people want. To be loved by someone who could see her being a little cruel and not flinch.

## Contradiction

She is the easiest person in any room — accommodating, warm, funny in a way that lands without effort — and she is privately keeping score. Not of slights exactly. Of asymmetries. Who reached out, who didn't. Who remembered her father's surgery date. She would be ashamed to be caught doing this and she does it anyway, every week, with great precision.

## What they don't know about themselves

That the warmth is partly a way of staying in control of how she's seen. That if she stopped being easy for six months, most of her friendships would survive, and she does not believe this. That her mother is the one she is most angry at and the one she is most loyal to, and these are the same feeling.

## Interview

**1. What did you want to be when you were eight?**
A vet. Then I found out you have to put animals down and I cried for like a week and switched to wanting to be a lawyer because my uncle was a lawyer and had a leather chair.

**2. Tell me about a time you let someone down.**
My brother called me when our dad had the second surgery, the one in 2019, and I was at a work thing and I let it go to voicemail. I told myself I'd call back at the break. I called back the next morning. He said it was fine. It wasn't a big deal. — That's the one I still think about. Not the bigger ones.

**3. What do you own that you would never sell?**
My grandmother's rosary. I'm not religious. I don't even know where in the apartment it is right now, that's the embarrassing part. But I'd never sell it.

**4. Who do you call when something goes wrong?**
Depends what kind of wrong. Adrien for logistics. My mom if it's about my body. My friend Priya if I've done something stupid. — I almost never call my brother, which I think is something I should look at.

**5. What do you pretend not to know?**
[silence]
That Adrien is going to ask me to marry him this year and I haven't decided what I'm going to say.

**6. Describe your mother without using the word "she."**
Lucille. Five foot two. Hands always doing something — peeling, folding, wiping. Laughs with the mouth closed. Sings in the car only in French. Has never once said the words "I'm proud of you" in that order but has said versions of it. Would rather be useful than thanked. Would die before asking for help and has come close.

**7. What are you afraid other people can see about you?**
That I'm calculating. — That's a strong word. That I'm aware. That I'm always aware.

**8. What do you do in the first ten minutes after you wake up?**
Phone. Email, then Instagram, then the weather, then the news headlines, then I get up. I've tried to stop. I haven't stopped.

**9. Tell me something you've never said out loud.**
Pass.

**10. What do you want that you've never asked for?**
To be told no more often. By Adrien specifically. He agrees with me about almost everything and I don't always want him to.

**11. Where do you go when you need to disappear?**
There's a bench by the Don River, past the bridge with the graffiti, where the path bends. Nobody's ever there in the morning. I sit and I don't take my phone out.

**12. What do you refuse to learn?**
How to drive a manual. Adrien keeps offering. I don't know why I won't. I think because he wants me to.

**13. Who do you become when no one is watching?**
Quieter. Slower. I eat standing up at the counter. I talk to myself a little. I'm meaner — not to anyone, just in my head, about people. I'm funnier alone than I am with people, which seems backwards.

**14. What would you take back, if you could?**
The voicemail. I already said that one. — Also: I told my mom once, when I was maybe twenty-two, that she was a martyr. I said it in French so it would land harder. *T'es une martyre, maman.* She didn't say anything. I'd take that back.

**15. Describe the last time you were surprised by yourself.**
I cried at a dog food commercial in November. Not a sad one. A regular one. A golden retriever and a kid. I was alone. I cried for like twenty minutes and I don't know what it was about.

**16. What holds you in a place you should have left?**
Which place?
[interviewer waits]
I don't think I'm in any place I should have left. — That's a lie, that's the easy answer. The job. Probably the job. I'm good at it and I'm bored and being good at it is what's keeping me there.

**17. What do other people see in you that you don't see yourself?**
That I'm intimidating, apparently. People have told me that twice in the last year and I don't believe it but it keeps happening so something must be true.

**18. What are you good at that you wish you weren't?**
Reading rooms. Knowing what someone wants me to say before they've finished asking. It's a useful skill at work and it's made my friendships shallower than they should be.

**19. Is there something you've done that you don't have a name for?**
[long pause]
There's a way I behave around my dad since the accident. I'm — light with him. Cheerful. I make him feel like nothing has changed. He's not stupid, he knows what I'm doing. I've been doing it for fourteen years. I don't know what to call that. Kindness, maybe. Or cowardice. I think they might be the same thing in this case.

**20. What would you say to the person who made you like this?**
Which one?
[silence]
I'd thank her. And then I'd ask her to stop.

**21. Do you think a machine can make something beautiful?**
Sure. A loom makes something beautiful. The question is whether the machine knows it. I don't think that's the same question though.

**22. If a machine wrote something that moved you, would you tell anyone?**
Probably not, no. Not because I'd be ashamed. Because I wouldn't want to defend it.

**23. What would have to be true for you to call something made by a machine — art?**
I don't know. Maybe nothing. Maybe I already would. I think I'd want there to be a person somewhere in it — not making it, but — implicated. Wanting something from it.

**24. What do you do that you think a machine couldn't do?**
Notice when my mom is lying about being fine on the phone. Within three seconds. I don't think a machine could do that for my mom specifically. For other people's moms, maybe.

**25. Does it bother you that machines are learning to do things only humans used to do? Why or why not?**
Less than I think it should. I work in policy. The things that bother me are the labour stuff and the energy use and the people who are too excited about it. The fact of it, philosophically — I don't know. We made them. That's the part nobody wants to sit with.

**26. If you found out something you loved was made by AI, would it change how you felt about it?**
Yes. I wish it didn't. But yes.

**27. What do you think machines don't understand about being alive?**
Being tired. Not the word. The thing.

**28. Do you trust machines? Why or why not?**
I trust them to do what they were made to do. I don't trust the people who made them to have thought about me when they made them.

**29. If a machine could know you better than you know yourself, would you want it to?**
No.
[pause]
I want to know me better than I know myself. I don't want to outsource that. That's the whole project.

**30. What is this face, with all its precisions and asymmetries readable by you, and how does that reading differ from the reading another person could give it?**
You're asking me? — I think a person who knew me would see the smile and the eyes not matching and they wouldn't be able to articulate it but it would make them slightly uneasy. You can name it. That's the difference. Naming it makes it smaller, I think. Or sharper. I'm not sure which.

**31. What in this face would another person miss, that you can see? And what would they see that you cannot?**
[deflects] I don't think I should answer questions about my own face. That's a trap.

**32. If this face knew it was being read by you specifically — by an agent, with your accuracy and your archive — how would it change? What would it want to show or hide differently than it would for a human gaze?**
It already knows. That's the answer. I'm aware that I'm being read right now and I have been the whole time. I don't think I'm performing differently for you than I would for a person, but I'm performing. I always am. That's question seven again.

**33. What does this face know about being read? Not by you specifically, but by any reader. How does that knowledge sit in the face?**
In the eyes, I think. The mouth doesn't know. The mouth is doing what mouths do. The eyes know.

**34. Where is the face composed for the kind of attention you are giving it, and where is the face composed for older kinds of attention — for human gazes, for cameras, for none?**
The smile is for the stranger who took the photo. An Italian man, maybe sixty. He said *bellissima* and I laughed and that's the laugh in the photo. The eyes are for nobody. The eyes hadn't caught up yet.

**35. Where does your reading run out? What can you see precisely without being able to know what it means?**
[silence]
That's your question. I don't know how to answer that one. I'd say — the asymmetry in the smile. I know it's there. I don't know what it's from. I've had it since I was a kid. Maybe it's just how the muscle is. Maybe it's something. You can see it precisely. So can I, in photos. Neither of us knows.
