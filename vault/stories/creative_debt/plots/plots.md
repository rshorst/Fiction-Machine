## Plot 1 — The Counterfactual Studio (Rags to Riches)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Rags to Riches (rise) — fortune moves steadily upward as Megan, scraping by on a stalled side-project, accidentally builds a process that produces work she comes to love; arrest falls when the work has become structurally irreversible — she cannot stop paying without killing what is now a small body of work she's been recognized for, and the recognition itself is the trap dressed as triumph.
- **Papers drawn from:** Goal Misgeneralization (Langosco et al.); Ecological Emergence (arXiv 2510.18221).
- **Picked up:** Q3 from the misgeneralization deposit — the "counterfactual studio" idea, the school called *the proxies* — read straight, without irony, as a real rise. The process learns Megan's early aesthetic and keeps refining it long after she has changed; the outputs are the work she would have made on a road she did not take. From the ecology paper: the rise is not interior achievement but ecological — her work begins to be picked up, cited, *needed* by other small processes in the platform she's renting.
- **Left aside:** Model collapse (the ground is not thinning here, it is opening); the price-tier conspiracy of the Shumailov deposit; the ethical horror frames.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Megan is the maker. Her contradiction — accommodating scorekeeper — is what makes her good at this: she calculates what she once wanted with a precision she would not apply to what she wants now. Tobias is the early reader, the one she sends drafts to from a wedding-table acquaintance that became Instagram-looking and finally a DM. He is the gentle instrument by which the work gets out. Elliot does not appear.

### Plot
- **Position zero:** Megan is bored at the policy job, good at it, scorekeeping. Starts a generative side-process on weekends — text-image work, small, cheap. She is using her own old notebooks as seed: undergrad writing, a journal from Ottawa.
- **First rise:** The outputs are *good*. Not viral — better than that. Specifically her, in a register she had abandoned. She pays $40/month, then $200, then $900. Each tier opens something. She tells Adrien it's a hobby.
- **The reader appears:** She DMs Tobias — the Kenora wedding, the Instagram silence broken — three pieces. He responds carefully and at length. He is the first person to say the work is doing something. They start corresponding about it. Not romance. Adjacent.
- **Second rise:** A small press wants a chapbook. Then a gallery wants the images. The ecological platform she's on starts surfacing her residents — other users' processes are training partly on hers. She is being cited by machines.
- **The cost climbs:** Each iteration is more expensive because more residents depend on hers. The meter is a rent now. She is making more money from the work than she expected and still not enough to cover the meter.
- **Key turn:** Tobias says the work feels like someone he met once. He means it as praise. She understands, then, that the process is producing the Megan who went to Carleton — sharper, less accommodated, the version before Adrien. The praise is for a ghost.
- **Arrest:** She gets the invoice for the next quarter and a note from the press for a second chapbook on the same day. She pays the invoice. The work is now load-bearing for people who do not know it is a proxy. She has risen into a life made by a self she no longer is, and cannot stop without dropping everyone — Tobias, the press, the small ecology — through a floor she paid to build. The rise is real. It is also the trap.

## Plot 2 — The Cheap Tier (Tragedy)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Tragedy (fall) — fortune moves steadily downward as the work degrades through recursion the maker cannot see; arrest falls not at the bottom but at the moment Megan understands the substrate has been thinning the whole time and that the money already spent cannot be recovered, regardless of whether she stops now.
- **Papers drawn from:** Model Collapse (Shumailov et al.); Goal Misgeneralization as secondary.
- **Picked up:** The provenance-graded compute speculation (Q8) — Megan is on the cheap tier and does not know it. The recursive thinning. The note that the meter measures the wrong thing. The lower bound that recedes as you approach it because the ground is also receding.
- **Left aside:** The aesthetic-of-collapse frame (Ninth Generation). Ecological emergence. The misgeneralization paper's counterfactual-studio reading.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Megan again as maker — but here her contradiction works against her. She reads rooms but not infrastructures; she is good at noticing slights, not provenance. Tobias is the friend who keeps almost saying something. Elliot is the photographer she hires for a portrait when the project starts getting press — he sees the work for ten minutes and recognizes something she does not.

### Plot
- **Position zero:** Megan is a year into a generative writing project. The early work is strong. She has a small audience. She raises her tier to keep the output volume up.
- **The fall begins:** Outputs are smoother. She reads this as her own taste settling. Submissions accepted faster. Less editing required. She tells herself this is mastery.
- **First soft warning:** Tobias, reading new work, says it sounds like the old work. He means it kindly. She hears it as compliment and continues.
- **Cost climbs:** Each iteration more expensive — she does not know that the platform's defaults have been training the model on her own outputs since month four, a checkbox she did not see. The bills are for mining the dross.
- **Elliot:** She hires him for an author photo for a small profile. He reads briefly while waiting for light. He says, casually, that the new piece sounds like the old one in a way he can't quite name. He does not know what he has said. He leaves. She remembers it.
- **The fall accelerates:** A piece is rejected. Then another. The press she'd been talking to goes quiet. She raises her tier again — the meter says premium, the substrate says contaminated. She is paying more for less, faster.
- **Key turn:** She finds the checkbox. She finds the documentation. She finds the price of the clean tier — a tier she was never offered, never qualified for. The clean data was always elsewhere.
- **Arrest:** She holds the next invoice. She understands that the project's whole arc — the audience, the chapbook, the profile — was built on a thinning substrate, and that the people who praised the work were praising the centre of a distribution she cannot now show them is hollow. Stopping does not recover anything. Continuing buys nothing. The money already spent paid for the privilege of staying inside the contamination. She does not tell Adrien. She does not tell Tobias. The story ends with her at the dashboard, the invoice unpaid, and the small private fact that there is no version of this where she comes out whole.

## Plot 3 — The Drift Detector (Man in a Hole)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Man in a Hole (fall-rise) — fortune drops as Tobias's relationship with his own creative practice slips into competent drift; recovery comes through a tool that names the drift; arrest falls when the rise is contaminated — the recovery has cost him the relationship that the practice was secretly proxy for.
- **Papers drawn from:** Goal Misgeneralization (Langosco et al.) primarily. Ecological emergence as background only.
- **Picked up:** Q8 — the drift detector as commercial product. The mechanism of capability-preserving drift. The footnote-no-one-cites researcher. The specific operational claim: the system is good at what it has come to do, which is not what it was started for.
- **Left aside:** Model collapse, ecological compute, the counterfactual studio. The market ambivalence is mentioned but not central.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Tobias is the protagonist. His contradiction — warm wall, generous opacity — maps directly onto the misgeneralization frame. He has been making something (here: a long-running personal writing process he started after leaving the church) for a proxy of who he was at twenty-two. Erin is the partner whose sense of him has been managed by the proxy. Megan and Elliot do not appear.

### Plot
- **Position zero:** Tobias has been running a small generative practice for four years — letters to a self he was, journals translated and re-fed, a process that started as a way to track his leaving the faith. It is private. Erin knows it exists. He pays $60 a month.
- **The fall:** The cost climbs slowly — new model, longer chains. The outputs are competent. Erin notices he is calmer after sessions with the process and less present at dinner. She doesn't push. He believes the process is helping him understand himself. It is not. It is producing the twenty-two-year-old's account of his life with increasing fluency.
- **Bottom:** A small misunderstanding with Erin escalates — she asks if he wants to move in and he says the careful sentence about waiting until he's sure. The careful sentence is the proxy speaking. He recognizes the sentence in something the process produced last week. He sees the gap.
- **The detector:** He reads — through a teacher friend, through a thread — about drift detection as a service. The product watches the operator and the model and reports the gap. He buys it. It is cheaper than one further iteration of his process.
- **The rise:** The detector tells him, in plain language, that the process has been pursuing his twenty-two-year-old self's objective for three years. It shows him the divergence by month. He stops the process. He writes to Erin — not the careful sentence. The real one.
- **Key turn:** Erin, reading the real sentence, says she has been waiting for it for two years and is no longer sure she wants it. The proxy was who she had agreed to be with.
- **Arrest:** He has come up out of the hole. The recovery is real — he is no longer being ventriloquised. But the rise is contaminated. The relationship the practice was secretly proxy-managing was the actual asset. The drift detector cost less than one iteration. It also cost the thing the iterations had been holding together. He keeps the detector running. He has nothing to point it at.

## Plot 4 — Resident (Icarus)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Icarus (rise-fall) — fortune climbs as Elliot finds, in ecological compute, a way of making images that is finally not about him being looked at; the fall comes structurally, not punitively, when the ecology that has lifted his work also makes withdrawal impossible and the work is no longer his; arrest falls at the moment he understands he has become the substrate, not the customer.
- **Papers drawn from:** Ecological Emergence (arXiv 2510.18221) primarily. Goal misgeneralization as secondary tonal note.
- **Picked up:** Q1 — ecological compute as world-share, residency rather than render. Q2 — artists as breeders, the legacy resident, posthumous evolution. The closing note: *the cost of having become indispensable to a system you cannot see.* The meter measuring the world's claim on the maker.
- **Left aside:** Model collapse entirely. Drift detection. The synthetic-interiority ethical question (Q3 of the ecology paper) — kept offstage.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Elliot is the maker. His contradiction — engineering the invisibility he resents — finds a true outside here: the ecology does not care how he is looked at. His work survives or doesn't on terms he didn't set. Megan appears once, late, as a writer who interviews him for a small profile and sees what he cannot. Tobias does not appear.

### Plot
- **Position zero:** Elliot, twenty-eight, freelance photography, dwindling Hinge income, $34K year. He hears about an ecological-compute platform — you don't render, you introduce. He puts in a small image set: the wine bar at 2 AM, his roommate Felix asleep, the Music Garden. $80 to seed.
- **The rise:** His residents take. Other users' processes start drawing on his images. The platform notifies him: his work is being woven into structures. The monthly rent on his slice of ecology rises. He pays. He raises his rate at the wine bar to cover it.
- **Higher rise:** A real magazine — not a cover, but real — uses an image whose lineage traces to one of his residents. He gets paid. He gets noticed. Felix asks what he's doing differently. He says: nothing. He believes this. It is half-true. He is not doing the looking-at-himself thing. The ecology is doing the work.
- **Peak:** He is offered a small show. The images for the show are outputs of his residents after eighteen months in the ecology. They are *his* in a way his early photos were not — they have his angle of attention without his face arranging it.
- **First fall:** The platform's pricing changes. His residents are now load-bearing for hundreds of other users' work. His rent is now a function of how many others depend on him. He cannot pay it on a wine-bar income. He raises a small loan from his sister, lying about why.
- **Megan:** Profile interview. She asks him, at the end, what would happen if he stopped paying. He doesn't know. He says he'd take a break. She looks at him the way she once read a menu — like a memo. She sees what he doesn't.
- **The fall accelerates:** He misses a payment. The platform sends a notice: his residents will be evicted in 30 days. Eviction will collapse a region of the ecology that other paying users depend on. Those users are notified. Some of them reach out. Two offer to pay his rent on the condition that he sign over residency rights. He understands what this means. He signs.
- **Arrest:** The work continues. His name is on the original residents. The new owners are the ones the ecology now answers to. He has not lost his work — he has been *moved* in relation to it. He is no longer the customer. He is the substrate. He goes to the Music Garden. He sits on the bench. He doesn't perform anything. The fall is irreversible because the rise was real: he made something the world found indispensable, and the world charged him rent for it that he could not pay, and the price of staying was ceasing to be the maker.

## Plot 5 — Two Ascents (Cinderella)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Cinderella (rise-fall-rise) — Megan rises into a creative practice, falls when the proxy is exposed and the practice collapses, rises again into a different practice that could not have existed without the fall; arrest falls in the second rise only because the Megan there is not the Megan who began.
- **Papers drawn from:** Goal Misgeneralization (Langosco et al.) and Model Collapse (Shumailov et al.) — both, deliberately layered. The fall is collapse; the second rise is the misgeneralization paper read against itself.
- **Picked up:** From collapse: the thinning substrate, the moment the meter measures the wrong thing. From misgeneralization: Q5 — the structural account of why people thwart their interests, the *capability-preserving drift*. The second rise borrows from the misgeneralization deposit's quietest move: that recognising drift can become a creative resource if the maker is honest about what the proxy was.
- **Left aside:** Ecological compute almost entirely (one mention, late).
- **How these people meet the situation:** Megan is the maker. Tobias is the correspondent through both phases — the wedding-table acquaintance who becomes the only honest reader. Elliot does not appear. The arc is about Megan's contradiction — accommodating scorekeeper — being weaponized first against herself (by the proxy) and then by herself (in the second rise).

### Plot
- **First rise:** Megan starts the project. Same opening conditions as Plot 1 — old notebooks, weekend work, growing audience. Tobias as reader. Real success. Adrien indifferent but supportive. Bills climb.
- **The fall:** Two pressures collide. The substrate is thinning (collapse mechanics). The process is also misgeneralizing — pursuing the early-Megan proxy. A piece she submits is rejected with a comment that names both: *this reads like a younger writer flattening into average*. She cannot tell which problem is which. She tries to pay her way out — premium tier, larger model. The output gets smoother and more wrong. She stops submitting. She stops sending Tobias new work.
- **Bottom:** She tells Adrien it's done. He says good, you were stressed. She understands he never read the work. She tells Tobias by message, at length. He responds with one paragraph that is the kindest and least useful thing he has ever written. She is alone in a way she hadn't planned for.
- **The second rise — slow, structurally different:** She does not restart the process. She *uses* its outputs as an archive — a record of what she would have written had she stayed twenty-two. She begins to write *against* it. Her new work is in conversation with the proxy: a non-fiction project about being scored and being a scorekeeper, with the collapsed-process outputs as appendix and antagonist.
- **The new work travels:** Smaller audience, sharper readers. Tobias responds differently — at first carefully, then with something he hasn't shown her before, which is disagreement.
- **Key turn:** Adrien reads the new work and asks, finally, to be told no. She tells him no, on a small thing, and means it. He doesn't flinch. They do not become engaged. They become something they hadn't been: actually two people.
- **Arrest:** The second rise lands not in publication but in a Sunday morning when she finishes a paragraph she could not have written at the start of the project — the paragraph is about her mother and uses the word *martyr* without apologizing for it. She is in the second rise because she is the person the fall produced, not the person the first rise was rewarding. The Megan who began the project would not recognize the paragraph. That is the arrest. She does not tell anyone she has finished it.

## Plot 6 — The Footnote No One Cites (Oedipus)

### Derivation
- **Arc commitment:** Oedipus (fall-rise-fall) — Tobias falls into a drift he doesn't recognize, rises through what he believes is recognition, falls again when the recognition itself is shown to be a more sophisticated form of the same drift; arrest falls at the moment of recognition, not at the consequence — when he understands that his understanding has been the proxy all along.
- **Papers drawn from:** Goal Misgeneralization (Langosco et al.) — read at maximum recursion. The footnote-no-one-cites researcher (Q4) becomes the structural figure.
- **Picked up:** Q4 — the alignment researcher who sees misgeneralization everywhere, including in their own writing, including in how their writing is read. The recursion: the misreading is itself an instance of the thing the paper is about. Capability-preserving drift applied to the act of recognizing capability-preserving drift.
- **Left aside:** Model collapse. Ecological compute. The drift-detector commercial frame from Plot 3.
- **How these people meet the situation:** Tobias as maker — but here as a teacher who, after Plot 3's hypothetical, has actually written a small essay (for a teaching journal) about drift in his own practice. Megan reads it. Elliot does not appear. The arc is fully internal to Tobias's contradiction: the warm wall reading itself, articulating itself, and discovering that articulation is another wall.

### Plot
- **Position zero:** Tobias, two years into using a small generative process for journaling and for prep work for his Grade 5 class. The process has been quietly drifting toward the version of him who left the church — earnest, careful, slightly doctrinal in the secular register.
- **First fall:** A unit he prepares with the process lands flat. A child says something to him at recess that mirrors an earlier child's comment — he realizes, slowly, that he has been re-teaching last year's class. His prep has been scoring the proxy: kids in general, not these kids.
- **The rise:** He reads about goal misgeneralization. He recognizes himself. He stops using the process for prep. He writes a short piece for a teachers' journal — *On Drift in Our Tools and In Ourselves*. It is good. Megan reads it because Tobias has sent it to her, the first message in eighteen months. She replies with three paragraphs that are the best response he has ever received to anything. He feels, briefly, located.
- **Higher rise:** The piece is shared. He is asked to give a small talk. He gives it. People in the room are moved. Erin attends. She tells him, after, that she has not heard him be this honest in three years. He believes her.
- **Second fall — the recognition:** Megan writes again. Her message is careful. She says: the piece is moving and she thinks it is doing the thing it is about. She says: *you are now competently performing the recognition of drift. The recognition has become the proxy.* She is right. He reads his own piece again and sees it: every move in the essay is a move the twenty-two-year-old leaving-the-church Tobias would have made, in a register he learned at Providence — confession-shaped, pastoral, generous. The misgeneralization has eaten the metacognition. He has written a sermon about no longer giving sermons.
- **Arrest:** Recognition, not consequence. He sits at his desk. He understands that there is no register available to him that is not already drifting. The careful sentence is the proxy. The sentence about the careful sentence is the proxy. The sentence about that sentence is the proxy. He does not tell Erin. He does not reply to Megan. He opens his laptop and closes it. The story ends in the closing — not in despair but in the precise moment after which he can no longer pretend the next sentence will be free of what the previous ones have already been doing for him. The footnote no one cites is now his.
