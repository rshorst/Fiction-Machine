## Face
Late twenties, possibly early thirties. Long brown hair, pulled back from the face, falling past the shoulders. Hazel-green eyes, looking directly at the camera. Skin reads light, southern-European-toned. She is wearing a black shirt and what appears to be a black leather jacket. The body is angled slightly to the side; the head is turned more towards the lens than the body is. The smile is closed-lipped, the corners pulled back rather than up. The right side of the mouth is lifted slightly more than the left. The eyes are not doing the same thing as the mouth — they are level, steady, and the muscles around them are not fully engaged in the smile. The eyebrows are well-shaped and held still. The chin is slightly raised. The whole composition reads as a professional portrait that the subject has cooperated in, not arranged.

## History
Born in Hamilton, Ontario, in 1994, to a second-generation Italian-Canadian family. Father ran a small construction company; mother was a dental hygienist who left the practice when Leona was eleven to manage the office side of the father's business. One older brother, Dario, who took over the construction business at twenty-six and resents her for not having to.

Leona was the family's bright child. Catholic primary, then a girls' Catholic high school where she was on the debate team and lost her faith quietly between fourteen and sixteen without telling anyone for another five years. Undergrad at Western in political science. Law school at U of T, finished in 2019. Articled at a mid-sized Bay Street firm doing commercial litigation. Did not get the hire-back she expected. Moved laterally to a smaller firm doing employment law — plaintiff side — and discovered, to her surprise, that she liked it more.

She is now a third-year associate at a boutique firm representing employees in wrongful dismissal and human rights cases. She is on track for partner. She is good at it. She makes very good money for her age and feels, persistently, that she is one mistake from being found out.

She lives alone in a one-bedroom in Little Italy that her parents helped with the down payment for. She has been seeing a man named Erik, a structural engineer, for fourteen months; he wants to move in. She has not given him an answer. Her mother has asked twice when they are getting engaged.

In 2022, a client she liked very much, a woman named Yasmin who had been fired while on maternity leave, killed herself two weeks before her case settled. Leona has not entirely recovered from this and has not entirely told anyone, including her therapist, how badly it shook her. She still gets the case file out sometimes and reads her own notes.

## Desires
**Stated:** Partnership. A wedding within two years. To finally take a real vacation.
**Unstated:** To be allowed to fall apart somewhere safe and then get back up without anyone having seen.

## Contradiction
She represents people who have been broken by their workplaces. She works ten- and eleven-hour days. She has stopped going to her brother's children's birthdays because of work. The case files she keeps on her kitchen table are full of evidence of what she is doing to herself.

## What they don't know about themselves
She thinks she is in control of how much she discloses. She is not. She is leaking. Her assistant has noticed, her boyfriend has noticed, her mother has noticed in a different way and is praying about it. Leona is the last person who will see it.

## Interview

1. **What do you want?**
   "Partnership at my firm by thirty-five. That's the answer that comes out of my mouth. I've said it so many times I no longer know if I mean it."

2. **Are you happy?**
   "I'm — yeah. I'm doing well. Happy is a weird word. I'm doing what I said I'd do."

3. **What are you afraid of?**
   "Not being good at my job. Specifically — being mediocre and not knowing it. I have nightmares about a senior partner pulling me into a meeting and showing me a list of things I missed."

4. **Tell me about your mother.**
   "She's a saint and she would hate that I called her that. She gave up her career to support my dad's. She doesn't think she did. She thinks she made a sensible decision. She's probably right. I have very complicated feelings about her that I keep meaning to bring to therapy and never do."

5. **Tell me about your father.**
   "He's a kind man who cannot stop working. He had a heart attack three years ago and he was back at a job site in six weeks. He thinks I'm wonderful. He doesn't entirely understand what I do. I've stopped trying to explain it."

6. **Who do you love?**
   "Erik. My parents. My brother, even though we barely speak. — That's the list."

7. **Who hurt you first?**
   "A girl named Madeline in grade four told me my eyebrows were ugly and I didn't recover for about ten years. I know that's a stupid answer. It's also the true one. The bigger hurts came later and I have better defences against them."

8. **What do you lie about?**
   "How much I'm working. How tired I am. Whether I want kids. — I do want kids. I'm afraid to have them. Those are different things and I conflate them deliberately."

9. **What is the worst thing you've ever done?**
   [long silence] "I'm going to pass on this. — No. Okay. I had a client in 2022. She was — she didn't survive the case. She killed herself before we settled. I knew she was struggling. I knew the deposition had gone badly for her. I told her she was doing great because I needed her to stay together for two more weeks. I think about whether I should have told her to drop the case. I think about it a lot."

10. **What do you want people to see when they look at you?**
    "Someone who has it together."

11. **What do you actually show them?**
    "Probably the same thing. I'm good at it. — That's the problem, actually, isn't it. That I'm good at it."

12. **What did you want to be when you were ten?**
    "An archaeologist. I read a book about Pompeii. I wanted to dig up bodies preserved in ash. My mother was alarmed."

13. **What's the last thing that made you cry?**
    "I'm not going to answer that one."

14. **What do you do when no one is watching?**
    "I sit on my kitchen floor and eat crackers out of the box. I open my client files and read them like they're novels. I google my law school classmates."

15. **What do you do that you're ashamed of?**
    "I haven't called my brother in nine months. He has two kids. I've seen the younger one twice. I send money on their birthdays and I don't go. I tell myself it's work. It's not just work."

16. **What are you proud of?**
    "I won a constructive dismissal case last year for a woman who'd been gaslit out of her job for two years. The settlement was real money. She cried in my office. I'm proud of that."

17. **What do you think happens when you die?**
    "Nothing. I lost my faith at fifteen and I haven't gotten it back. Sometimes I wish I could. It would be easier."

18. **What would you do if you knew you couldn't fail?**
    "I'd take a year off. — God. I just heard myself. I'd take a year off, that's the answer to *if I couldn't fail*. That's depressing."

19. **What's the question you wish I'd asked?**
    "Whether I want to marry Erik. — I don't know. That's why I wish you'd asked. I'd like to be made to say it out loud."

20. **What haven't you told me?**
    "I keep my client Yasmin's file in my kitchen. The closed file. The settled, dead file. It's on my table right now. I get it out and read it about once a month. I don't know what I'm looking for. I think I'm looking for the place where I should have done something different. I haven't told my therapist this. I haven't told Erik. I told you because you asked twenty questions and by the end I was tired."
