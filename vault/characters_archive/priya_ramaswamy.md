## Face
Late twenties. The face is composed in a way that does not appear to be for the camera. The eyes are level and slightly asymmetrical — one fractionally narrower than the other — and the gaze is not inviting and not closed. The mouth is at rest but there is a small tension at the corners, as if a fuller expression started and was held back. Hair pulled tight away from the face, no earrings visible. Jacket open, no visible necklace, no decoration. The choice is to remove decoration and present the face. She is not smiling. She is also not refusing to smile. The stillness is the dominant fact.

## History
Born in Mississauga, 1996. Parents from Chennai, both arrived in Canada in the late 1980s. Father a chartered accountant; mother taught grade three for twenty-six years and is now retired and very bored. One younger brother, Arun, who is in med school in Halifax and the family's preferred topic of conversation.

Priya was the kind of student who did everything well and nothing brilliantly. McMaster for undergrad — health sciences. Then law school at Osgoode, which she chose partly because she wanted to and partly because it foreclosed the question of what to do. She articled at a mid-sized Bay Street firm doing corporate work, accepted the hire-back, and is now in her second year as an associate. She works between sixty and ninety hours a week. She is on track. Everyone says she is on track.

She broke off an engagement nine months ago. His name was Rohit. He was kind. The families had met. She did not tell anyone she was going to do it before she did it. She sat him down on a Sunday in March and said the sentence she had been rehearsing for six weeks and he cried and she did not. Her mother has not been the same with her since.

She has been seeing a therapist on Wednesdays at 7am, before work, for fourteen weeks. She has not told her parents. She has not told her brother. She has not told the therapist some things either.

## Desires
**Stated:** To make partner. To buy a condo on her own, in her own name, in the next two years. To see her brother graduate.
**Unstated:** To be unrecognizable to the people who know her. To wake up in a city where no one knows her parents' names.

## Contradiction
She is the most reliable person anyone in her life knows, and she is rehearsing — privately, daily, for years — the act of disappearing.

## What they don't know about themselves
That breaking the engagement was not the beginning of anything. It was the only thing she has done in her adult life that came from herself rather than from the shape of what was expected. She thinks of it as a failure. It was the opposite.

## Interview

1. **What is your name, and is it the one you were given?**
Priya Ramaswamy. Yes. My mother wanted to name me Lakshmi after her mother and my father said it was too much to put on a child. I think he was right. Priya means "beloved." That's also a lot, but it's quieter.

2. **Where do you live, and is it where you mean to be?**
I rent a one-bedroom in Liberty Village. It's fine. I'm not there much. I don't know if it's where I mean to be. I mean to be somewhere. I haven't decided where.

3. **What did your mother do?**
She taught grade three at the same school for twenty-six years. She knew every child's name and every child's parents and every child's situation. She was very good at it. She retired two years ago and now she calls me four times a week.

4. **What did your father do?**
He's a CA. He still works. He'll work until he can't. He doesn't have hobbies. That's not a criticism. He just doesn't.

5. **What are you good at that nobody pays you for?**
[pause] I'm good at — this is going to sound strange. I'm good at sitting with people who are upset. Not fixing it. Just sitting. I did it for my brother for years. He had panic attacks in high school. I would just sit on his floor.

6. **What are you bad at that you wish you weren't?**
Wanting things. I mean — wanting things specifically. I can want things in the abstract. I can want a good life. I can't want, like — that restaurant, that vacation, that dress. I can't pick.

7. **When did you last cry?**
Three weeks ago. In the bathroom at work. I don't remember why. I remember it was after a partners' meeting but it wasn't about the meeting.

8. **What do you lie about?**
[long pause] I tell my mother I'm dating someone. I'm not. I told her I was, after — after I broke off the engagement, I told her about six months later that I was seeing someone new. I'm not. I don't know why I said it. I keep updating it. He has a job now, in my version. He's a consultant.

9. **Who have you not forgiven?**
Myself. Is that the answer you want? It's also true. I haven't forgiven myself for — for hurting Rohit. I don't think I will. I don't think I'm supposed to.

10. **Who has not forgiven you?**
Rohit's mother. She sent me a text two weeks after. It was four sentences. I deleted it. I remember all four sentences.

11. **What is the worst thing you've ever done?**
I let it go on too long. With Rohit. I knew in October. I did it in March. Those five months are the worst thing.

12. **What is the best thing you've ever done?**
Same answer. I did it.

13. **What are you afraid of?**
That I'm going to be exactly who I am right now in twenty years. That nothing else is coming.

14. **What do you want that you have not told anyone?**
[silence]
[silence]
I want to move. Not within the city. Out. I don't know where. Somewhere I'd have to start over with everything, including groceries. I want to not know where the grocery store is.

15. **What do you do when no one is watching?**
I look at apartments online in cities I've never been to. Lisbon. Edinburgh. Montreal, even, which isn't far enough. I have a folder.

16. **What is something you believe that most people you know do not?**
That you can love someone and still need to leave them, and the loving doesn't make the leaving wrong. Most people I know think those two things cancel out. They don't.

17. **What is something most people believe that you do not?**
That family is the thing that holds you together. I think family is one of the things that can hold you together. It's also one of the things that can hold you in place.

18. **Describe a room you remember.**
My grandmother's house in Chennai. The room where she slept. There was a ceiling fan that was too loud and a green bedspread and a framed photograph of my grandfather, who I never met, on the wall. The window had bars on it and a curtain that didn't reach the bottom. I slept on the floor next to her bed when we visited. She talked in her sleep. In Tamil. I didn't understand most of it. I understood my mother's name.

19. **If you could go back, would you?**
To when?
[interviewer: to any point]
No. There isn't a point I'd go back to. There are points I'd skip if I could.

20. **What question were you hoping I wouldn't ask?**
Number fourteen. You noticed.
