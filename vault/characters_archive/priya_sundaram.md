## Face
A wide smile, teeth visible, the cheeks pushed up into firm rounds. The smile is asymmetric — the right corner pulls higher than the left. The eyes do not match the mouth. They are steady, level, looking directly at the camera, and they are not smiling in the same register; they are watching. There is a slight forward lean of the upper body. The hair is dark, pulled back loosely, a few pieces falling forward near the temple. She is wearing a plain grey t-shirt with a crew neck. The skin reads warm-toned, South Asian. Late twenties, possibly early thirties. The background is undifferentiated, indoors, not arranged. Nothing about the framing is performed for status. The face is held open, but the eyes have not given anything away.

## History
Born in Chennai, moved to Mississauga at nine, when her father took a contract job at a pharmaceutical firm and the family followed six months later. Her mother had been a vice-principal at a girls' school in Tamil Nadu and never worked again after the move; she developed a serious gardening habit and a quieter, steadier resentment that Priya only recognised as resentment in her twenties.

Priya was the older of two — a brother, Karthik, four years younger, the family's announced genius, now a cardiologist in Boston. Priya was the family's announced "people person," which she came to understand meant: the one who would manage everyone else's feelings.

She did her undergrad in psychology at McMaster, then a Master's in industrial-organisational psych at Guelph. She works now at a mid-size HR consulting firm in downtown Toronto, doing organisational culture assessments — surveys, interviews with employees, reports to executives about why their people are unhappy. She is good at it. She is the one clients ask for.

She lives in a one-bedroom in Leslieville with a cat named Beans. She had a five-year relationship with a man named Marcus that ended eighteen months ago when he told her, on a Tuesday, that he had never felt fully himself with her, and she realised, sitting on the kitchen floor afterwards, that she agreed. She has not told her parents they broke up. They think she is "still figuring things out with him." She sends a photo of the cat at every major holiday.

She runs three times a week along the Don Valley. She sees a therapist named Annette every other Wednesday and lies to her about how often she drinks wine alone.

## Desires
**Stated:** To make senior consultant within two years; to "be more present" in her relationships; to visit her grandmother in Chennai before it's too late.
**Unstated:** To be known by someone without having to do the labour of presenting herself; to stop being the one who manages the room; to be allowed to be the difficult one for once.

## Contradiction
Her job is to elicit honest disclosure from strangers. She is famously good at it. People in her interviews tell her things they have not told their spouses. She has not told her therapist of three years that her brother once, at fifteen, told her she was the reason their mother was so tired.

## What they don't know about themselves
She thinks she is warm. She is warm. She is also keeping score. She has a list, not written but very precisely maintained, of every person who has failed to ask her how she is doing in a way that suggested they wanted a real answer. Marcus was on the list eight months before she let herself notice.

## Interview

1. **What do you want?**
   "Professionally? I'd like to be running my own practice in five years. Personally — god, that's a bigger question. I'd like to feel less tired."

2. **What are you afraid of?**
   "Becoming my mother. That's the fast answer. The slower one — I'm afraid I've already chosen the kind of life she had and I just haven't admitted it."

3. **What do you do when no one is watching?**
   "I eat standing up at the counter. I talk to my cat in a voice I'd be embarrassed about. I reread old text messages from my ex and try to figure out exactly when it went wrong, like there's going to be a specific text where I can point and say *there*."

4. **Who do you love?**
   "My brother. My grandmother. Beans. [pause] I'm noticing I didn't say my parents. That's interesting. Can we move on?"

5. **What do you lie about?**
   "How much I drink. How often I think about Marcus. Whether I like my job. I do like my job. I don't always like that I'm good at it."

6. **What is the worst thing you've ever done?**
   "I told my brother, when he was about twelve, that our mother had wanted to abort him. I made it up. He cried for a long time. He's never mentioned it to me as an adult and I don't know if he's forgotten or just decided not to bring it up. I think about it more than is probably healthy."

7. **What do you want people to see when they look at you?**
   "Someone competent. Someone you'd want in the room when something difficult is happening."

8. **What do you actually show them?**
   [silence] "I don't know how to answer that without sounding either falsely modest or weirdly arrogant. Pass."

9. **Who hurt you first?**
   "My mother. Not on purpose. She was unhappy and she was the only adult in the house who had time for me, so I learned to manage her unhappiness instead of having my own. I don't blame her. I'd like to blame her. It would be cleaner."

10. **What did you want to be when you were ten?**
    "A vet. Then a lawyer, briefly, because I watched a lot of *Law & Order*. By thirteen I wanted to be a therapist. I am, sort of. I do organisational therapy."

11. **What's the last thing that made you cry?**
    "A dog food commercial. Genuinely. The dog was old. I'm not going to elaborate."

12. **Tell me about your mother.**
    "She was a vice-principal in India. She gave that up to come here. She gardens. She is more interesting than she lets anyone be around for. She has a way of asking how you are that makes you not want to tell her. Next."

13. **Tell me about your father.**
    "He's a kind man who was not present for most of my childhood and is now retired and trying to be present and I don't know what to do with him. He calls me on Sundays. We talk about the weather in two cities."

14. **What do you do that you're ashamed of?**
    "Already answered, I think. The brother thing."

15. **What are you proud of?**
    "I'm good at my job. I'm good at it in a way that is not just performance. People are actually a little less unhappy because of work I've done. That's not nothing."

16. **What do you think happens when you die?**
    "Nothing. I think it's just nothing. I find that comforting, actually, which I know is supposed to be a weird thing to find comforting."

17. **What would you do if you knew you couldn't fail?**
    "Stop. I'd stop. I think I'd just — not work for a year. I'd go to Chennai. I'd sit with my grandmother. That's the answer. I notice I had to think about it for a long time."

18. **What's the question you wish I'd asked?**
    "Whether I'm happy. I'd have liked the chance to dodge it."

19. **Are you happy?**
    "[laughs] You're not playing fair. — I'm functional. I have a good life by most measurements. I'm not unhappy. That's the most honest thing I can say."

20. **What haven't you told me?**
    "I haven't told my parents that Marcus and I broke up eighteen months ago. They think we're still together. I send them updates about a relationship that doesn't exist. I don't know how to stop. Every Sunday I tell my father what Marcus and I did on the weekend and I make it up. It's the longest sustained lie of my life and I can't tell if I'm protecting them or punishing them."
