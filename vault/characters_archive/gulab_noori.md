## Face
A man in his late sixties or early seventies. White hair, full white beard, neither trimmed close. Skin warm-toned, reddened, with deep horizontal lines across the forehead and pronounced creases running from the nose to the corners of the mouth. The skin has the texture of long sun exposure. He is wearing a red vest over what appears to be a lighter-coloured shirt. He is looking directly at the camera. The gaze is steady and does not blink away. The mouth is closed and the lips are pressed together — not tightly, but held. There is no smile. There is no frown either; the face is at rest, but the rest is not relaxed. The eyes do not match the apparent stillness of the mouth — they are doing more work. The eyebrows are slightly drawn together, the inner ends very slightly raised. Features read Central or South Asian, possibly Afghan or Hazara, though the photograph is not conclusive.

## History
Born in 1953 in a village outside Mazar-i-Sharif. Father was a small landholder who grew almonds and ran a few sheep. Gulab was the third of seven children, the second son. He went to school until he was fourteen and then was needed at home. He learned to read well enough; he learned to do sums very well. He liked the orchard work better than the sheep.

Married at twenty-two to a woman named Shahnaz, chosen by his mother. They had four children — three boys and a girl — between 1977 and 1986. The Soviet years passed and his older brother was killed; Gulab inherited responsibility for two households. The civil war years were worse. He learned to keep his face still in the presence of armed men of any affiliation, because any expression at all could be wrong.

In 1998 he took his family across the border into Pakistan, then to Iran, then back. They returned to a different village. His daughter, Farida, was married off at seventeen to a man Gulab did not entirely trust; she lives now in Hamburg with three children of her own and Gulab has not seen her in person in eleven years. His youngest son, Hamid, was killed in 2011 — a roadside bomb, not aimed at him specifically. Gulab does not say his name aloud anymore. He says it inside his mouth sometimes when he is alone.

He came to Toronto in 2022 with his wife, his eldest son's family, sponsored under a special programme. He lives in a two-bedroom apartment in Thorncliffe Park with six other people. He works part-time at a halal grocery in the back, stocking and unloading. He is paid in cash. His English is enough for the work and not enough for anything else. He prays five times. He walks every afternoon along Overlea Boulevard and counts the buses.

## Desires
**Stated:** For his grandchildren to have proper schooling. For his wife's knees to stop hurting. For his daughter to visit.
**Unstated:** To be useful in a way that is not symbolic. To do one piece of physical work that matters before he dies. To not become an old man who is moved from room to room.

## Contradiction
He is the patriarch of his household and is consulted on every important decision. He is also the only person in the apartment who does not know how to use the washing machine, and he has never asked, because asking would mean admitting that the machine has been there for two years and he has been wearing clothes washed by his daughter-in-law for two years, and he has feelings about this that he cannot name without dishonouring her kindness.

## What they don't know about themselves
He believes he survived because he was careful. He survived because he was lucky. The carefulness came after, as a way of explaining the luck to himself. His youngest son was also careful.

## Interview

1. **What do you want?**
   "I want my wife's knees not to hurt. I want my grandchildren to learn properly. After that — I am an old man, what should I want."

2. **What are you afraid of?**
   "Becoming a burden. [pause] No — being one already and not knowing it."

3. **Tell me about your mother.**
   "She was small. She had a very loud voice for someone so small. She knew the names of every plant in our valley. She died in 1989. I was not at home. This is a regret."

4. **Tell me about your father.**
   "He was a serious man. He hit us when we were boys, but not often, and not with anger — with a kind of duty. I did not understand him until I was a father. Then I understood him too well."

5. **Who do you love?**
   "My wife. My children. [pause] I am not going to count them for you."

6. **Who hurt you first?**
   "[silence] — A teacher. When I was small. It is not important. He is dead a long time."

7. **What do you lie about?**
   "I tell my wife my back does not hurt. She tells me her knees do not hurt. We have an arrangement."

8. **What is the worst thing you've ever done?**
   "[long silence] I will not answer this. Ask another."

9. **What do you want people to see when they look at you?**
   "Nothing in particular. I would like them not to look at me with pity. That is all."

10. **What do you actually show them?**
    "An old man with a beard. They see what they want."

11. **What did you want to be when you were ten?**
    "A teacher. I liked the man who taught us mathematics. I thought, this is a good life, to know the answer and to give it to a child. — Then I became someone who unloads boxes. This is also a life."

12. **What's the last thing that made you cry?**
    "I do not cry in front of others. — Alone, last week. A song on the radio in the shop. An old song. Not Afghan, Iranian. It does not matter."

13. **What do you do that you're ashamed of?**
    "I do not know how to use the washing machine in my son's apartment. I have lived there for two years. My daughter-in-law washes my clothes. I have not asked her to teach me. This is shameful."

14. **What are you proud of?**
    "My children are alive. Three of them. This is not nothing. In my generation this is not nothing."

15. **What do you think happens when you die?**
    "I know what happens. God is merciful. I am not afraid of this part."

16. **What would you do if you knew you couldn't fail?**
    "I would plant an orchard here. In this country. I know it is not the climate. — You said if I could not fail."

17. **What's the question you wish I'd asked?**
    "You should have asked about my son. The youngest one. Then I would have refused to answer, but you should have asked."

18. **Tell me about your son.**
    "[silence] [silence] His name was Hamid. That is what I will tell you."

19. **What do you do when no one is watching?**
    "I count things. Buses, mostly. I walk on Overlea and I count the buses going east and the buses going west. When the numbers are equal I go home. It is foolish. I know it is foolish."

20. **What haven't you told me?**
    "I have not told you that I am not certain I want to be here. In Canada. I came for my grandchildren. They are doing well. The reason I came is being fulfilled. And still every morning I wake up and for one second I do not know which country I am in, and in that second I am always at home, and then I remember, and the second ends. I have not told my wife this. I will not. She is happier here. That is enough."
